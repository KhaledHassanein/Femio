R1.4 — onboarding reference

The moment Femio's whole promise is made. Today it is a title, a heading, a subheading, options, a labelled progress bar and a button — six levels of hierarchy on step one, which reads as a form. It should read as being asked about. Built on assets/brand/tokens/femio.tokens.css, imported. Approve or reject before implementation (docs/25 §1).

The flow — EN, light

Four steps. One question each.

The hardest step, in the other three states

Triage is where this screen can fail: up to fifteen conditions, special-category data, and the one question a woman might close the app rather than answer. Shown in dark, and in Arabic RTL in both themes.

What changed, and why

1 · "Welcome to Femio" is gone

It was the app talking about itself on the one screen that should be about her. The wordmark stays in the top bar — she knows whose app it is — and the space goes to the question.

2 · One question per screen, and it is the biggest thing on it

Step one currently carries a title, a heading, a subheading, four options, "Step 1 of 4", a progress bar and a button. Here the question is Fraunces at --fs-xl with one supporting line, and everything else is subordinate. Same information, one hierarchy.

3 · Progress is four dots, not a labelled bar

A filling bar with "Step 1 of 4" is the visual grammar of a form — a tax you are being asked to complete. Dots say the same thing quietly. The active dot elongates rather than changing colour alone, so it is not a colour-only signal.

4 · Triage is grouped, and the escape is visible

Fifteen chips in a flat list is a wall, and this is the question most likely to make someone close the app — it is special-category data about conditions she may not have told anyone. So: quiet group labels, 44px chips, the non-diagnostic note kept directly under them, and "None of these apply" as a visible button rather than a link she has to find. Skipping must look as legitimate as answering, because under product rule 2 it genuinely is.

5 · The last step shows the experience, not a summary

This is the most important change. The confirm step currently recaps what she typed. Instead it renders what Femio will actually lead with — her hero card, the modules that will be surfaced, and the promise that nothing is hidden. It is the payoff for having answered, and it is the first thing a competitor's onboarding does not do.

It is also an architectural marker: that preview is ExperienceConfig.home rendered (docs/24 §2). When R2 lands, this screen should be reading the real document rather than a mock — which makes onboarding the natural first consumer of the engine.

6 · The first botanical line-work

docs/25 §5a committed to single-weight botanical line-work from the Bloom mark instead of stock portraiture, and nothing had drawn any. The bloom on the final screen is the first. Single stroke weight, no fill, one colour, inherits --primary so it themes for free and mirrors in RTL without a second asset.

Known gaps

  • Not verified in a real browser. The template was executed in node — no undefined output, correct state count — but the pane would not composite, so the visual judgement is the owner's. Before implementing, run axe and check the AR bidi at 390px: chips mixing Arabic labels with Latin abbreviations are where bidi mangling shows up.
  • The preview is a mock. It hardcodes a cycle-mode result. Wiring it to the real resolver is R2 work, not R1.
  • Copy is a draft and reuses the shipped strings where they were already good — the triage intro and non-diagnostic note are unchanged, because they are the best copy in the product.
  • Account step not redesigned here. It is step 3 and mostly fine; it inherits the one-question-per-screen frame.

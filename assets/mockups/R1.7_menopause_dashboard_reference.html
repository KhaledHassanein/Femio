R1.7 — the menopause dashboard: a count, and no conclusion

docs/25 §3.5 names the brief exactly: this screen reports a count and refuses a conclusion, and that has to read as respect rather than as evasion. Everything below is an answer to that one sentence. Drawn as modules, like R1.6 and R1.5, because R2 makes the dashboard an ordered list the engine chooses.

Same honesty note as R1.5. registry/ holds tracks A, D and E. Track F has no rows yet, so the module lists here are read from F1/F2/F4/F6 §I and are a specification for Track F (filed as R2.9), not a copy of engine output. What the app already ships — the months-since count, the convention sentence, the variability card, the MRS and the medication list — is drawn as it actually behaves, and this reference says where each of those moves.

1 · The module inventory

Track F has the widest emotional range in the product: the same inventory has to serve a 47-year-old whose cycles are lengthening and a 34-year-old whose ovaries were removed last month.

2 · Assembled — four states of one transition

F1 and F2 differ in whether a cycle exists to describe. F4 differs in what the hero is for — orientation rather than arithmetic. F2 + a bleed is the one state where a red panel outranks everything on the screen.

3 · The hardest states

F6 discreet, which has to be safe on a glanced screen — and F4 in Arabic, which is the hardest copy in this track for the same reason E6 is in Track E.

What this decides

1 · The count is a fact; the twelve-month mark is a convention that nothing points at

The current panel already refuses to draw a bar filling toward twelve months, and the reasoning holds (ADR-042/043): the clinical convention is twelve months with no other cause, and pregnancy, thyroid disease and several medicines also stop periods. Drawing progress toward a conclusion Femio may not reach turns arithmetic into a diagnosis. This reference keeps that and fixes the consequence — removing the ring left the screen with nothing but text, which is the flatness R1 exists to fix.

2 · What replaces the ring is the shape of the transition, without its destination

menopause.trend draws her recent cycle lengths as columns — 31, 24, 41, skipped, 38, 62 — with skipped cycles as dashed outlines rather than gaps, because a gap reads as missing data and a skipped cycle is data. It shows lengthening and scattering, which is what F1 §I asks the copy to say, and it points at no endpoint. A woman can see her own transition without being told where it ends.

3 · The MRS shows a number and her own trend, and no word

P2.4 shipped it that way in code — no band, no severity label, two tests asserting none appears — and the design problem that creates is real: a number with no label looks unfinished. The answer here is that her own previous totals are the context, drawn as a small strip beside the number. "22, and it was 17 in June" is information she owns. "Moderate" is an assessment, and the woman it hurts is the one who reads it and decides the appointment can wait.

4 · Postmenopausal bleeding is the only red panel in Track F, and it outranks the hero

F2 §I: any post-FMP bleed pins the red-flag box to the top, above everything. This is the one place in this track where Femio routes rather than educates, and two rules travel with it: it carries no phone number (P1.26.1, global-only), and it is never attributed to dryness — F6 §I is explicit, because the GSM content is the most plausible wrong explanation a woman could reach for on her own.

5 · F4's hero is orientation, not a count

For induced menopause the months-since number is not the question; "what has happened to me and what does it mean" is. So the hero is anchored on the onset she gave, names the thing plainly, and the grief and identity module sits directly under it rather than at the bottom of the screen. The three sentences F4 §I forbids are forbidden here too — never "the same as natural menopause", never "at least you're alive", never a countdown. This is the tonal sibling of E6, and it is drawn in Arabic for the same reason.

6 · Discreet mode hides presentation, never access

F6's card is deliberately dull: a plain surface, no imagery, wording that survives being read over her shoulder on a bus. The lock line is stated on the card rather than hidden in Settings, because a discreet mode she cannot see is one she will not trust. And it hides nothing from her: the same content is reachable from search whether the mode is on or off, which is product rule 2 in the one place people expect an exception.

7 · There is no HRT card, because there is no HRT list

P2.4 shipped medications as one list — levothyroxine, a patch, iron and a prenatal vitamin belong together — and an HRT-shaped module here would rebuild the restriction-by-data-structure that decision avoided. The module shows what she is taking and when she started, and holds the two absences the code holds: no dose guidance and no adherence score. Stopping something moves it down the list; it never deletes the row.

8 · "Years since your final period" is a fact. "You are postmenopausal" is a conclusion.

F2's hero says the first. The word postmenopause appears on this screen only as the name of a chapter of content, never as a status Femio has assigned her. That is the whole difference the brief asks this screen to make legible, and it is checkable in a screenshot.

Known gaps

  • Track F has no registry. Filed as R2.9. F3 (comorbidity) and F5 (premature / early) are not drawn — F5 in particular deserves its own pass, because the tone problem there is closer to F4's than to F2's.
  • The CBT-informed program is content, not code (P2.13b), so the module that would surface it is a placeholder row rather than a designed surface.
  • Not composited in a browser here — executed in node, coverage counted, no undefined leaking. The visual judgement is the owner's.
  • Arabic is a draft in the same sense as R1.5's: it follows the approved menopause strings rather than being newly reviewed. F4's Arabic is the paragraph to read hardest.

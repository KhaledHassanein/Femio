What changed, and why

The shipped homepage is a hero sentence over five visually identical link cards — a sitemap with a headline. It shows no product, gives no reason to trust, and says nothing a competitor could not say. Seven changes, each with a reason you can argue with.

1 · The life-stage rail is the page

The one claim no competitor can make is continuity — Flo covers cycles, Ovia hands you between three apps, Balance starts at 45. The rail states all six stages in one glance, with the phase-colour spine running through it, and the line under it answers the question that actually stops people signing up: what happens to my data when my life changes? It re-layers; it never deletes (product rule 5).

2 · Honesty is shown, not claimed

"Honest predictions" is an adjective anyone can type. So the page draws the band with its fading edges, prints the real no-late-counter copy, and shows thirty days of one woman's flow. Three product behaviours a competitor would have to change their product to copy.

3 · The anti-claim sits in the hero

Flo carries an FTC settlement, Premom carries another, and What to Expect was flagged by Mozilla. "Does not diagnose, never sells your data, and both are checkable" is the strongest sentence Femio owns — and it is also the regulatory boundary, so it earns its place twice.

4 · Trust is five verifiable facts, not badges

Named clinician with credentials · linked guideline citations · EU residency · in-app export and delete · no channel to an advertiser. Every one is already true and already implemented, which is why none of them needed softening.

5 · Type finally does its job

Fraunces is loaded and was being spent on card titles. Here it carries only the statements. In Arabic the pairing is by weight — IBM Plex Sans Arabic at semibold, one step larger, with --lh-arabic — because there is no serif counterpart and faking one would be worse than not pairing.

6 · One raised element

The hero is the only thing carrying a gradient and --shadow-md. Everything else is resting or sunken. That is the whole fix for "cards of equal weight", and you can check it in a screenshot.

7 · --gradient-dawn finally gets a job

Violet → raspberry → amber has been in the token file since day one, unused. It becomes the marketing signature — homepage, app icon, store assets — and is never used inside the signed-in app, which stays calm.

Known gaps in this reference, stated rather than hidden

  • No illustration yet. docs/25 §5a commits to botanical line-work from the Bloom mark. None is drawn here — the layout is designed to survive without it, so it is an upgrade rather than a dependency.
  • Two defects were found by rendering this, not by writing it. (a) The Arabic headline silently fell back to Georgia — Fraunces carries no Arabic glyphs, and the !important on the display rule beat the RTL font rule. (b) The first draft put white body copy on --gradient-dawn, which ends at amber-500 — white there is ~2.1:1, a clear AA failure. Dawn is now a 5px signature bar carrying no text, over an AA-safe violet field. Both are exactly the class of bug docs/22 §10 says a diff cannot show you.
  • The rail's phase-colour spine is decorative. Colour is not the only channel — every stage is named in text — but it must not gain meaning later without a legend.
  • Copy is a draft. The English needs a claims-lint pass and the Arabic needs the P1.29 human reviewer before it ships.
  • R1.1 is still owed. This was designed from the token file, the persona specs' §C teardowns and the shipped app — not from a week of using Flo, Clue and Ovia on a phone. Expect it to revise some of this.

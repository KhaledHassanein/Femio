R1.3 — homepage reference

Four states, judged at 390×844. Built on assets/brand/tokens/femio.tokens.css — imported, not copied — so nothing here can specify a colour, radius or shadow the product cannot build. Scroll each phone. Notes below explain every decision, so the argument can be against a specific choice. Approve or reject before any of it is implemented (docs/25 §1).

What changed, and why

The shipped homepage is a hero sentence over five visually identical link cards — a sitemap with a headline. It shows no product, gives no reason to trust, and says nothing a competitor could not say. Each change below has a reason you can argue with, and several are now backed by the competitor teardown in docs/03 §H.

1 · The life-stage rail is the page

The teardown found that not one of Flo, Clue or Balance markets continuity — Flo sells scale, Clue sells science, Balance sells a clinician, and Ovia splits its lifecycle across three apps. The rail states all six stages in one glance, and the line under it answers what actually stops people signing up: what happens to my data when my life changes? It re-layers; it never deletes (product rule 5). This is the one claim a competitor cannot answer by copying a screen.

2 · A product screenshot, because the market says so (R1.3a)

Clue leads with a phone mockup of its own UI; Balance does the same with abstract geometry. Neither uses a mood image. The first draft of this reference had no product in it at all — the teardown called that its biggest weakness, so the real dashboard now sits directly under the hero: gradient hero card, eyebrow → statement → band → uncertainty line, one accent action, bottom tab bar. It is drawn from the same tokens as the app, so it cannot drift from what ships.

3 · Honesty is shown, not claimed

"Honest predictions" is an adjective anyone can type. So the page draws the band with its fading edges, prints the real no-late-counter copy, and shows thirty days of one woman's flow. The teardown makes this sharper than expected: the top reported complaint against the market leaders is that predictions degrade badly for PCOS and perimenopause, and one is reported to assume day-14 ovulation regardless of logged data. Femio's rule 3 was written before we knew that. It is the strongest product answer on the page.

4 · The anti-claim sits in the hero

Flo's own App Store description answers privacy with a certification — the 2025 PICCASO ISO 27001 award, in paragraph two. Femio's answer is structural: there is no channel from health data to an advertiser, by construction. A structural claim beats a badge, and it is also the regulatory boundary, so it earns its place twice.

5 · Trust is five verifiable facts, not badges

Named clinician with credentials · linked guideline citations · EU residency · in-app export and delete · no channel to an advertiser. Balance puts its clinician's post-nominals on the page and it is the most credible thing on their site — Femio can do the same the moment P1.9.7 is unblocked.

6 · Type finally does its job, and one raised element

Fraunces was being spent on card titles; here it carries only the statements. In Arabic the pairing is by weight — Plex Sans Arabic at bold, with --lh-arabic — because there is no serif counterpart and faking one would be worse. The hero is the only thing carrying a gradient and --shadow-md; everything else is resting or sunken. That is the whole fix for "cards of equal weight", checkable in a screenshot.

7 · --gradient-dawn finally gets a job

Violet → raspberry → amber has been in the token file since day one, unused. It becomes the marketing signature — homepage, app icon, store assets — and never appears inside the signed-in app, which stays calm.

8 · Applied after review (R1.3b, 2026-08-11)

The clinician-review trust line is gone. It read "Every guide is reviewed by a clinician — named, with credentials, on the page", and that is not true today: bylines are PENDING on all 30 imported drafts (P1.9.8) and the reviewer bio is blocked on P1.9.7. On a YMYL homepage that is a false medical-review claim, which is precisely what claims-lint exists to catch. It is replaced by what is verifiable right now — cited, dated sources — and can be restored the moment P1.9.7 lands.

The hero now says what the app is. "Your health has one story" is good prose and poor comprehension; someone arriving from a search for "period tracker" needs the noun. This is also the SEO landing page.

The language section was folded into trust, taking the page from eight sections to seven. I also reversed my own recommendation to cut the 30-day strip card — I had called it a weaker restatement of the band, but it is not: the band is about uncertainty and the strip is about your pattern versus a population average, which is exactly the opening docs/03 §H.2 found (predictions collapsing for PCOS and perimenopause). Cutting it would have removed the strongest competitive point on the page.

Known gaps, stated rather than hidden

  • Two defects were found by rendering this, not by writing it. (a) The Arabic headline silently fell back to Georgia — Fraunces carries no Arabic glyphs and the !important on the display rule beat the RTL font rule. (b) The first draft put white body copy on --gradient-dawn, which ends at amber-500 — ~2.15:1, a clear AA failure. Dawn is now a 5px bar carrying no text over an AA-safe violet field (8.98:1 and 5.70:1). Exactly the class of bug docs/22 §10 says a diff cannot show you.
  • No illustration yet. docs/25 §5a commits to botanical line-work from the Bloom mark. The layout is designed to survive without it, so it is an upgrade rather than a dependency — and the teardown confirmed none of the three competitors uses stock portraiture either.
  • Copy is a draft. The English needs a claims-lint pass; the Arabic needs the P1.29 human reviewer before it ships.
  • R1.1b is still owed. The teardown's desk half is done; the in-app craft questions — one-handed logging, speed, haptics — need the owner's screen recordings.

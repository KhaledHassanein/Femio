R1.8 — track / log: the tools list, rendered

The most-repeated interaction in the product, and docs/25 §3.6's reason for designing it: every second it costs is paid daily. The finding that shapes this reference is structural rather than visual — see the box.

ExperienceConfig.tools already exists, is already served, and this screen ignores it. LogKind is a real union of ten values in lib/experience/types.ts, every persona record declares its tools, and track-screen.tsx branches on isPregnancy three times instead — the same write-only shape docs/23 §3.1 opened this workstream with, one screen later. So this reference draws the log screen as the tools list rendered through a logger registry, exactly as R2.3 did for the dashboard's modules. Filed as R2.10.

Two smaller consequences fall out of it and are drawn here: the BBT/OPK/mucus logging that shipped with P2.1 has no LogKind, so the engine cannot surface it and the dashboard reaches it through a mode === "ttc" button; and postpartum feeding logs (P2.3) have none either.

1 · The logger inventory

One drawing per LogKind, plus the screen furniture. Each has an open form and a collapsed row, because which one it gets is an emphasis the engine sets, not a property of the logger.

2 · Assembled — the same screen, five vocabularies

Cycle, pregnancy, postpartum, teen and menopause. The date strip, the recent row and the save bar are identical in all five; everything between them comes from tools.

3 · The hardest states

First run with nothing logged, an offline save sitting in the queue, and pregnancy in Arabic dark — where the urgency tiers, the counter and the calendar all have to mirror.

What this decides

1 · The screen is tools, rendered — not a mode branch

Same argument as R2.3's module registry, one screen later: with a branch, a new persona means editing this file, and the day someone adds a track and forgets it, a woman gets the wrong vocabulary at the moment she is trying to record something. With a registry she gets what her record says she has. The three isPregnancy branches in track-screen.tsx are the same defect persona-branch-lint was built to stop, surviving in a file its baseline still covers.

2 · One logger is open; the rest are 52px rows

Today every logger is expanded, so the most common action — one period tap, or two symptom chips — sits below a screenful of controls she is not using. The engine already ranks the tools; the ranking becomes open versus collapsed. B1 opens period. D1 opens the movement counter. F1 opens symptoms. The rest are one tap away and none of them is hidden, which is the difference between emphasis and restriction (product rule 2).

3 · The date strip is the spine, and it is sticky

One date for the whole screen (P1.28.3, already true) — but it currently scrolls away, so a woman logging three days of a period loses the only indication of which day she is writing to. Pinning it under the app bar costs 60px and removes the most likely wrong-day entry in the product. Days with a period logged carry a dot, so scrolling back is navigation rather than memory.

4 · Severity is optional and says so on the control

Every optional field that looks required turns a five-second interaction into homework, and B1 §A is explicit that she abandons apps that do this. The severity row carries the word optional, the save action is never disabled, and nothing anywhere counts how complete her logging is. No streaks, no percentage, no "you missed 3 days" — the same rule the medication list holds by test (P2.4), for the same reason: the apps that grade people on tracking make the worst days worse.

5 · The confirmation is the record appearing, and undo lives on it

No toast, no modal, no "are you sure". What she logged shows up in Just logged with an undo beside it, and it stays there for the session. A dialog to confirm a symptom chip is friction on the safe path; undo is recovery on the unsafe one, and it is the cheaper trade every time.

6 · Offline is a state of the row, not a banner

The IndexedDB queue already exists. A red bar across the top says the app is broken; a dashed chip reading saved on your phone, will sync says the one true thing — her entry is not lost. She is often logging in a bathroom, on a plane or in a hospital basement, and the design has to treat that as ordinary rather than exceptional.

7 · In pregnancy, the route to a human sits above the tool

P2.2.3 established this for the contraction timer and the movement counter, and this reference makes it a property of the screen: the maternity-contact panel is above both counters in every state. A woman opening the movement counter at 2am is already worried, and the tool must never be the thing between her and a person. The counter itself counts and shows elapsed time — no 5-1-1 verdict, no "normal" — because a phone telling her "not yet" is the failure with the worst consequence in the product.

8 · The teen screen is the same screen with plainer words

Not a reduced one. A2 §A: teen mode changes data handling, ads and social exposure, never knowledge — so the symptom vocabulary is the shared one, the severity control is the same control, and what differs is wording and the absence of the fertility tools she has no use for. A visibly simplified screen reads as being locked out, which A2 §B records in her own voice.

Known gaps

  • Fertility logging has no LogKind and neither does feeding. Both are drawn here as the loggers they would be, with their ids marked; adding the kinds is part of R2.10 rather than something to invent in a component.
  • Bleeding quantity for B4 is drawn as a facet of the period logger rather than a new kind. That may be wrong, and it is the one boundary in this file worth arguing with.
  • Not composited in a browser here — executed in node, coverage counted, no undefined leaking. The visual judgement is the owner's.
  • Motion is not shown. The signature (docs/25 §5a) is the prediction band drawing in, which belongs to the dashboard; this screen's motion is chip feedback at --dur-fast and nothing else.

# Fertility-awareness and evaluation safety source of truth

**Scope.** Global patient education for trying to conceive, pregnancy-test timing, and clinician-directed fertility evaluation. These rules are educational routing only: Femio does not diagnose infertility, order tests, interpret individual results, or promise conception.

## 1. Trying to conceive

- The fertile window is the six-day interval ending on ovulation day. Cervical-mucus tracking can help identify the window; basal body temperature (BBT) is mainly retrospective and should not be presented as a forward prediction or a guaranteed ovulation confirmation.
- Intercourse every 1–2 days during the fertile window gives the highest pregnancy rates, while 2–3 times weekly is nearly equivalent. Do not create pressure or imply that one missed day loses the cycle.
- Some lubricants reduce sperm movement in laboratory studies, but prospective studies have not shown reduced cycle fecundability among lubricant users. Do not quote the laboratory percentage as a real-world pregnancy effect. If a user wants lubricant while trying, suggest discussing or selecting a product specifically labelled for that use; do not endorse a brand.
- Moderate caffeine intake—about one or two cups of coffee per day—has no apparent adverse effect on fertility or pregnancy outcomes in the ASRM review. Avoid one universal milligram cutoff because drink size and caffeine content vary.
- WHO recommends **400 micrograms (0.4 mg) folic acid daily** from when a person begins trying to conceive through 12 weeks of pregnancy. Higher-dose regimens require an individualized clinician recommendation.

## 2. When to offer a fertility-evaluation conversation

- Begin evaluation at 12 months for a female partner under 35, at 6 months for age 35 or older, and consider more immediate evaluation over age 40.
- Do not wait for the duration threshold when there is a known condition associated with infertility, including irregular or absent cycles, suspected uterine/tubal/peritoneal disease or endometriosis, known or suspected male subfertility, or a treatment history that may reduce fertility.
- These are routing thresholds, not a diagnosis, prognosis, or failure line. Local referral pathways may differ.

## 3. Clinician-directed workup and ovarian-reserve information

- A fertility evaluation includes ovulatory status, female reproductive-tract structure and patency, and semen evaluation of the male partner where applicable.
- In people with regular 21–35-day cycles, added testing to confirm ovulation is generally not required unless there are other clinical indications. When a clinician orders a mid-luteal progesterone test, timing is individualized relative to the expected next period; Femio must not hard-code “day 21.”
- Ovarian-reserve tests are adjuncts in an infertility evaluation and help predict response to ovarian stimulation. They should not be used as screening tests in people who do not meet infertility criteria, and poor results do not necessarily imply inability to conceive naturally.
- Do not publish universal AMH, FSH, or antral-follicle-count cutoffs. Assays, laboratories, clinical context, and treatment protocols differ. Femio may store a clinician-provided result but must not label or interpret it.

## 4. Home pregnancy-test timing

- Pregnancy tests detect human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), which is produced after a fertilized egg attaches to the uterine wall. Testing too early can produce a false-negative result.
- Follow the current product instructions. For home urine testing, testing after the first day of a missed period is generally more accurate. If the result is negative and pregnancy is still suspected or the period remains absent, repeat according to the label or contact a clinician.
- Do not publish a universal “97–99%” accuracy promise or convert days-past-ovulation estimates into a guarantee.

## 5. Stress and emotional safety

- There is no conclusive evidence that higher stress reduces the chance of having a baby from fertility treatment. This evidence must not be expanded into a universal biological guarantee about every form of stress or natural conception.
- Ban blame-based language such as “just relax,” “stress is stopping you,” or “it will happen when you stop trying.” Offer coping support and clinical/mental-health routing without implying that anxiety caused infertility.

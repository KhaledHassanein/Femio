# Health-app claims and reproductive-data safety

**Applies to:** all Femio tracking, education, prediction, medication-reminder, laboratory-entry, partner-sharing, and condition-specific surfaces.

## Intended-use boundary

Femio’s current intended use is wellness tracking, patient education, organization of user-entered information, and preparation for conversations with qualified clinicians. It does not diagnose, stage, predict, rule out, treat, cure, or prevent a medical condition; interpret an individual result; select or dose treatment; replace professional care; provide contraception; or identify a day on which pregnancy cannot occur.

These boundaries reduce misleading-claim risk but do **not**, by themselves, establish regulatory classification in every market. Intended use, functionality, claims, evidence, and local law require review before launch and after material feature changes.

## Fertility-awareness and medication controls

- Every conception-prediction surface says: “Predictions are estimates, not guarantees, and are not a method of birth control.”
- Ban “safe day,” “infertile day,” “cannot get pregnant today,” pregnancy-avoidance efficacy, and instructions to stop or switch contraception.
- A medication reminder reproduces only a user-entered or authorized clinician schedule. No default or inferred dose, protocol optimization, interaction decision, missed-dose instruction, or “safe for you” statement.

## Store-policy baseline

- **Apple:** App Review Guideline 1.4.1 applies greater scrutiny to medical apps that may provide inaccurate information or be used for diagnosis/treatment; health-measurement accuracy claims require disclosed data and methodology, and users should be reminded to check with a doctor. Guideline 1.4.2 restricts drug-dose calculators.
- **Google Play:** the Health Content and Services policy requires a Health apps declaration, privacy policy, accurate sensitive-data disclosure, and non-misleading functionality. Non-device health/medical apps require the prescribed non-device disclaimer and clinician reminder; regulated devices must be declared and may require proof of authorization.

Re-check both official pages immediately before every submission.

## Reproductive and health data

- Do not send reproductive, sexual, pregnancy, loss, mental-health, laboratory, medication, cancer, genetic, or partner health data to advertising networks or use it for targeted advertising.
- Minimize collection; use clear just-in-time disclosure and granular, revocable permission; implement export, deletion, retention, security, processor, breach-response, and cross-border controls.
- Partner data requires the partner’s own informed authorization; one adult cannot consent on another adult’s behalf.
- Do not claim universal “GDPR/PDPL compliance.” GDPR Article 9 and national MENA laws require jurisdiction-specific legal-basis, transfer, and controller/processor assessment and a DPIA where required.

The FTC Flo (2021) and Easy Healthcare/Premom (2023) matters are design warnings: disclosures, SDK behavior, contracts, and actual data flows must match.

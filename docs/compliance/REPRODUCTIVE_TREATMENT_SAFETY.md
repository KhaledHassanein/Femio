## Recurrent pregnancy loss and early-pregnancy bleeding

- RCOG states that, for recurrent miscarriage associated with antiphospholipid syndrome (APS), clinician-managed low-dose aspirin plus heparin in pregnancy can improve the chance of a successful pregnancy.
- Progesterone is not a universal recurrent-miscarriage treatment. NICE NG126 limits its recommendation to a scan-confirmed intrauterine pregnancy with early-pregnancy bleeding and a history of miscarriage. Femio does not reproduce the dose or duration as a self-care instruction.
- Medication reminders are only for a regimen the user records as prescribed. The app does not infer APS, early-pregnancy eligibility, thyroid disease, or a treatment plan.

## Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS)

OHSS is a potentially serious complication of fertility treatment. RCOG advises contacting the fertility unit for symptoms, particularly pain that is not improving, vomiting, urinary problems, chest pain, or difficulty breathing. Severe features can include passing only small amounts of dark urine and a swollen/tender leg or chest symptoms associated with thrombosis.

Femio uses one plain-language action: contact the fertility clinic promptly for new or worsening possible OHSS symptoms; seek urgent local medical care for breathing difficulty, chest pain, a swollen/tender leg, severe or worsening abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, or markedly reduced/dark urine. It does not grade OHSS, tell the user to remain home, or calculate severity. The user's saved clinic contact and the global local-emergency fallback are displayed.

## Premature ovarian insufficiency (POI)

The 2024 international evidence-based POI guideline hosted by ASRM recommends clinician diagnosis based on disordered menstrual cycles for at least 4 months and FSH above 25 IU/L. It says to repeat FSH after 4–6 weeks if there is diagnostic uncertainty. NICE NG23 still describes elevated FSH on two samples 4–6 weeks apart and says not to diagnose POI from a single blood test. Both advise against routinely using AMH as the diagnostic substitute.

Femio therefore:

- identifies these as different current guideline protocols;
- never declares POI or menopause from a user-entered FSH/AMH result;
- does not tell a user to stop hormonal therapy to obtain testing;
- routes diagnosis and test interpretation to a clinician.

The international guideline recommends hormone therapy until the usual age of menopause to reduce morbidity and mortality, whether or not symptoms are present. NICE offers a clinician-directed choice of HRT or combined hormonal contraception unless contraindicated. Femio explains the replacement rationale and that HRT is not contraception, but supplies no dose, product, or personal safety decision. Public copy excludes unsupported cause percentages, a universal spontaneous-conception percentage, and prevalence ranges that vary by study.

Sources:

- [ASRM — Evidence-based guideline: Premature Ovarian Insufficiency (2024/2025)](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/evidence-based-guideline-premature-ovarian-insufficiency--2024/)
- [ESHRE — International Guideline on POI 2024](https://www.eshre.eu/-/media/sitecore-files/Guidelines/POI/2024/INTERNATIONAL-GUIDELINE-ON-POI_2024_2.pdf)

## Pregnancy-loss terminology and lactation

Miscarriage/stillbirth thresholds differ between countries and between clinical care, civil registration, and international statistics. For example, CDC uses 20 weeks in its U.S. public guidance, while WHO uses 28 weeks for international comparison and recommends using the national definition where relevant. Femio does not infer a legal/registration category or rank a loss; it uses the user's and care team's term and can explain that local definitions vary. Neonatal death refers to death of a live-born baby within the first 28 days.

After later pregnancy or infant loss, users may choose gradual lactation suppression, clinician-prescribed medication, continued expression, or milk donation where available. There is no single emotionally “correct” choice. Current NHS patient guidance describes supportive measures and clinician assessment for cabergoline, noting that it may not be suitable for everyone. Femio never recommends or doses cabergoline.

# Pregnancy and postpartum blood-pressure safety source of truth

> Global educational copy for Femio. It does not diagnose hypertension or pre-eclampsia, set an individual's medication plan, or replace maternity care.
>
> Medical reviewer: Mohamed K. · Review date: 23 July 2026 · Next review: 23 July 2027

## Measurement technique

Use a validated upper-arm monitor and the correct cuff size. Avoid smoking, caffeine, and exercise for 30 minutes beforehand; empty the bladder; rest quietly for at least 5 minutes; sit with back supported, feet flat and legs uncrossed; place the cuff on bare skin with the supported arm at heart level; do not talk; take two readings one minute apart and record both. A clinician should confirm that the device, cuff, and technique are appropriate.

Source: [American Heart Association — Home Blood Pressure Monitoring](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/understanding-blood-pressure-readings/monitoring-your-blood-pressure-at-home) (accessed 23 July 2026).

## Pregnancy reference points

- ACOG's April 2022 CHAP Practice Advisory, reaffirmed March 2025, recommends 140/90 mmHg as the threshold for initiating or titrating medication for chronic hypertension in pregnancy. It explicitly says this is a treatment threshold, not a goal blood pressure.
- NICE NG133 describes 140/90 mmHg or higher as hypertension in pregnancy, 160/110 mmHg or higher as severe hypertension, and 135/85 mmHg or lower as a treatment target. These are guideline reference points; they are not an app diagnosis.
- ACOG patient guidance defines severe hypertension in pregnancy as systolic 160 mmHg or higher and/or diastolic 110 mmHg or higher, and lists severe headache, vision changes, upper-abdominal pain, swelling of the face or hands, and difficulty breathing among warning symptoms that require prompt clinician contact.

Femio may display these as fixed educational references and route users to care. It must not label an entered reading, decide whether a user has pre-eclampsia, or give a start/stop/dose instruction.

## Aspirin wording

Do not publish one global aspirin dose as a Femio instruction. The USPSTF's 2021 U.S. recommendation specifies 81 mg/day after 12 weeks for people at high risk, while NICE NG133 specifies 75–150 mg daily from 12 weeks for certain risk groups. Femio may explain that clinicians sometimes prescribe low-dose aspirin for people at increased pre-eclampsia risk, but the user's clinician determines eligibility, product, dose, start, and stop dates.

Sources:

- [USPSTF — Aspirin Use to Prevent Preeclampsia, Final Recommendation Statement](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/document/RecommendationStatementFinal/low-dose-aspirin-use-for-the-prevention-of-morbidity-and-mortality-from-preeclampsia-preventive-medication) (28 September 2021)
- [NICE NG133 — recommendations 1.1.2–1.1.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng133/chapter/Recommendations) (75–150 mg from 12 weeks for specified risk groups)

## Postpartum follow-up

ACOG recommends blood-pressure evaluation no later than 7–10 days postpartum for people with a hypertensive disorder of pregnancy; those with severe hypertension should be seen within 72 hours. This is not “both checks for every user.” Symptoms can occur after birth: CDC urgent maternal warning signs apply during pregnancy and for one year after delivery.

Sources:

- [ACOG — Optimizing Postpartum Care, Committee Opinion 736](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2018/05/optimizing-postpartum-care) (May 2018)
- [CDC Hear Her — Urgent Maternal Warning Signs](https://www.cdc.gov/hearher/maternal-warning-signs/index.html) (15 May 2024)

## Global emergency routing

Femio asks for a maternity-unit contact and, where available, lets the user select their country to show the current official emergency destination. If that data is unavailable or stale, copy says: “Seek immediate medical care now. Contact your maternity unit or local emergency services. If you cannot reach them, go to the nearest emergency department.” Femio does not hard-code one country's number into global copy.

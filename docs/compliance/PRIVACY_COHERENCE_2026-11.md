Google sources read on 2026-11-01:
- AdMob "Google Play Data safety disclosure", https://developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy/play-data-disclosure — page shows "Last updated 2026-10-30 UTC", covers the Legacy Mobile Ads SDK 25.5.0. Lists four types, all collected **and** shared automatically, each for **advertising, analytics and fraud prevention**: IP address ("can help estimate a device's general location"), user product interactions (app launches, taps, video views), diagnostic information (app launch time, hang rate, energy usage), device and account identifiers (Android ad ID, app-set ID, other signed-in account identifiers where applicable). All encrypted in transit with TLS. Users can reset or delete the ad ID in Android settings. The developer is solely responsible for the Data safety answers.
- Play Console Help, "Provide information for Google Play's Data safety section", https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469 (latest change-log entry 5 Dec 2023): data sent off the device by an SDK counts as the developer's collection; inferred location (IP) must be disclosed; transfer to a service provider acting on the developer's instructions is not "sharing" (Google's ad serving for its own purposes does not fit that exemption, so "shared = yes" stands).
- Play Developer Policy Center, "User Data", https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888076 (no date shown beyond "2026 Google"): privacy policy link in the Console **and** "a privacy policy link or text within the app itself"; must name the developer, give a privacy contact, state the data types and the parties it is shared with, secure handling, retention and deletion; must be consistent with the Data safety section.
- Play Console Help, advertising ID, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6048248: apps targeting Android 13+ must declare `com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID`; the Mobile Ads SDK merges it in by default. The page does not say what happens if the permission is removed.
- AdMob UMP guide, https://developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy/gdpr ("Last updated 2026-10-30 UTC"): consent revocation is tied to the EEA, UK and Switzerland; `showPrivacyOptionsForm()` is the way to let users withdraw.

## 3 · Local law: what a no-server app with an ads SDK must still state

### Egypt, Personal Data Protection Law 151/2020
Source: unofficial English translation (the Official Gazette Arabic text is authoritative), https://acc.com/sites/default/files/program-materials/upload/Data%20Protection%20Law%20-%20Egypt%20-%20EN%20-%20MBH.PDF, read in full on 2026-11-01. Article numbers are those of the annexed law. The Executive Regulations (Ministerial Decision 816/2025) were **not** read: *not verified*; the repo's legal pack cites only law-firm summaries for them.

| What the text says | Why it matters for femio | Does the policy already cover it? |
|---|---|---|
| Art. 1: personal data includes an "online identifier"; "Sensitive Personal Data" includes physical or mental health data | The advertising ID and IP address the SDK sends are personal data. Her record is sensitive, but only if femio is a "controller" or "holder"; the on-device argument in `03_LEGAL_PACK.md` §1 is untested | Yes, in substance |
| Art. 2: personal data may not be collected, processed or disclosed "except with the explicit consent of the Data Subject or where otherwise permitted by law"; rights: know and access, **withdraw consent**, correct/delete, **limit processing to a purpose**, be told of a breach, **object** | The ad request is a disclosure of an online identifier to Google. In Egypt and Saudi Arabia Google's UMP form is not shown (Google ties its form to the EEA, UK and Switzerland), so the app has **no explicit consent step for ads there**. Whether Art. 6 conditions (consent; contract; legal duty; the controller's legitimate interest) cover this, and how the Centre treats an ads SDK in an on-device app, is *not verified* | Rights: partly (look, change, copy, delete). Withdraw / object / limit: **not stated** (P7) |
| Art. 3(1),(4): collect for "legitimate, specific, and transparent purposes"; do not retain longer than necessary | The policy must state the purpose of each flow and how long data is kept | Purposes yes; retention **no** (row 16) |
| Art. 4(9): the controller keeps a record of categories, recipients and cross-border transfers; Art. 4(10): the controller needs a Centre licence or permit | Internal record: the `DATA_MAP.md` and `PROCESSOR_REGISTER.md` files in `docs/compliance/` still describe the retired Supabase design and list no Google ads row. They should be replaced (see section 6). The licence question is the one the legal pack already flags | n/a (internal) |
| Art. 7: report a breach to the Centre within 72 hours; tell the data subject within 3 days after that | Needs a working breach route (`BREACH_RUNBOOK.md` exists) | not a policy item |
| Art. 14 and 16: moving personal data abroad needs an equal level of protection **and a licence or permit** from the Centre; Art. 15 lists the narrow cases where explicit consent can replace that (medical care, legal claims, contract, treaty and similar). Advertising is not in the list | The ad request leaves Egypt for Google's servers. The policy states this only for Vercel. Whether a permit is required for an SDK's ad request is *not verified* (the legal pack marks the same question for Vercel as [NEEDS SOURCE]) | Vercel yes; Google **no** (P6) |
| Art. 17: electronic marketing messages to a data subject need consent, the sender's identity, an address and an opt-out. "Electronic Marketing" is defined as messages "addressed to specific persons" | A non-personalised banner is not obviously a message addressed to a specific person; *not verified* how the Centre reads it | n/a |

The licence grace period: the legal pack (from CMS and Clyde & Co, not from the Executive Regulations' text) puts its end at 31 Oct or 1 Nov 2026, i.e. now. The owner decides whether Art. 4(10) or Art. 12 applies to femio; this note cannot settle it.

### Saudi Arabia, PDPL (Royal Decree M/19, amended M/148)
Official English text: https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/Personal%20Data%20English%20V2-23April2023-%20Reviewed-.pdf. **My fetch of it on 2026-11-01 returned "Request Rejected", so I could not re-read it.** The article references below are the ones the repo's legal pack recorded when it read the text on 2026-10-27, and they are **not re-verified today**. A secondary page I tried gave no article numbers and was not used.

| Duty (per legal pack, tag S1) | Policy today |
|---|---|
| Art. 12: a privacy policy available before collection | Present (website); not in the app (row 1) |
| Art. 13: notice to the person before collecting: who the controller is, how to reach them, the purpose, what is collected, who receives it | Controller name and email present. Purpose present. Recipients: Google (ads), Vercel (logs), Google Analytics and Meta (website, after consent) are named. The Saudi address detail of a controller representative is *not verified* |
| Art. 29 and the Transfer Regulation: transfer outside the Kingdom needs adequacy or safeguards and an impact assessment | Vercel yes, Google ads no (P6). The Transfer Regulation text was not read: *not verified* |
| Art. 20: breach notice to SDAIA | Not a policy item |
| Art. 2(2): personal-use exclusion protects **her**, not the developer | n/a |

A draft amendment to the Implementing Regulations that would add privacy-policy requirements was reported by Clyde & Co in October 2026 (https://www.clydeco.com/es/insights/2026/10/sdaia-publishes-draft-amendments-to-the-pdpl). It is a draft; watch it, do not rely on it.

**Plain answer.** The controller's identity and contact, the purposes, the rights, the complaint authorities and the Vercel transfer are in the policy already. What is still missing for an ads SDK is: (1) the full list of what the SDK sends and why; (2) that Google processes it under its own policy, possibly abroad; (3) retention; (4) the rights to withdraw and object; (5) a policy link inside the app. All are covered by section 4. What remains a **legal risk the owner accepts or removes** is the lack of an explicit consent step for in-app ads in Egypt and Saudi Arabia. Options, smallest first: (a) accept it and keep the text above, which tells her exactly what is sent; (b) add a one-screen "Show ads? Non-personalised; Google receives your advertising ID and internet address, never your records" choice at first use in countries where Google shows no form; (c) show ads only where Google's form applies. This is a product decision, not a verified legal requirement.

## 4 · Proposed edits (old → new). Apply after the owner signs.

The website edits are inside `apps/web/messages/{en,ar,fr,es}.json`, key `privacy`. Each "old" string is verbatim from the current catalogue. Arabic uses the feminine address as the file does.

### P1 · Encryption sentence (`privacy.points[1].body`, "What the app stores")
- **EN** old: `The file is protected by your phone’s own storage encryption. We do not add a second lock.`
  new: `The file is encrypted by femio, and the key is kept in Android’s secure key store (the Keystore), not beside the file. You can also switch on an app lock with a PIN.`
- **AR** old: `الملف محمي بتشفير التخزين في هاتفكِ نفسه، ولا نضيف قفلًا ثانيًا.`
  new: `يشفّر femio الملف، ويُحفظ مفتاحه في مخزن المفاتيح الآمن في أندرويد (Keystore) لا بجانب الملف. ويمكنكِ أيضًا تفعيل قفل للتطبيق برقم PIN.`
- **FR** old: `Le fichier est protégé par le chiffrement du stockage de votre téléphone. Nous n’ajoutons pas de second verrou.`
  new: `Le fichier est chiffré par femio, et sa clé est conservée dans le magasin de clés sécurisé d’Android (Keystore), pas à côté du fichier. Vous pouvez aussi activer un verrouillage de l’application par code PIN.`
- **ES** old: `El archivo está protegido por el cifrado de almacenamiento de tu propio teléfono. No añadimos un segundo bloqueo.`
  new: `femio cifra el archivo y guarda su clave en el almacén seguro de claves de Android (Keystore), no junto al archivo. También puedes activar un bloqueo de la app con un PIN.`

# Endometriosis content safety source of truth

**Scope.** Global patient education and symptom tracking for suspected or clinician-diagnosed endometriosis, including when fertility is a priority. Femio does not diagnose, stage, treat, or manage endometriosis; order or interpret tests; recommend treatment; compute the Endometriosis Fertility Index (EFI); or determine whether pain represents ovulation, endometriosis, or another condition.

## 1. Symptoms and tracking

- NICE advises considering endometriosis when one or more relevant symptoms are present, including chronic pelvic pain, period-related pain that affects daily life, deep pain during or after sex, cyclical gastrointestinal or urinary symptoms, or infertility with one or more of those symptoms.
- A pain and symptom diary may aid clinician discussions. Femio may record timing, location, severity, functional impact, and user-described associations, but it must not turn a pattern into a diagnosis or causal statement.
- Endometriosis symptoms can be persistent or recurrent and can affect quality of life. Avoid universal symptom-prevalence, mental-health-prevalence, dismissal, or diagnostic-delay figures in global user-facing copy.

## 2. Current diagnostic pathway

- Under NICE NG73 (updated November 2024), investigations, referral when indicated, and initial pharmacological treatment can proceed in parallel.
- NICE recommends transvaginal ultrasound for suspected endometriosis even when examination is normal. If it is declined or unsuitable, transabdominal pelvic ultrasound may be considered.
- A normal examination or ultrasound does **not** exclude endometriosis; referral may still be needed. Laparoscopy may be considered even when ultrasound or MRI is normal.
- Do not call laparoscopy the only “gold standard,” state that imaging rules endometriosis in or out, or imply that Femio can determine stage or diagnosis.

## 3. Education about care

- NICE recommends discussing benefits and risks of analgesics and allows a short trial of paracetamol or an NSAID for endometriosis-related pain. Hormonal and surgical options are clinician-directed and preference-sensitive.
- When fertility is a priority, NICE says not to offer hormonal treatment alone or with surgery to improve spontaneous pregnancy rates. Treatment decisions must account for the person’s symptoms, preferences, fertility priorities, ovarian reserve, and other clinical factors.
- Femio may explain categories and help users prepare questions, but it must not recommend an option, dose a medicine, rank surgery against assisted reproduction for an individual, or label an entry as safe or appropriate.

## 4. Fertility wording

- Endometriosis can be associated with difficulty becoming pregnant, but a diagnosis does not establish that an individual is infertile or predict whether natural conception will occur.
- Known or suspected endometriosis is a reason to begin fertility evaluation without waiting for the usual duration threshold. This is educational routing to a clinician, not a diagnosis or prognosis.
- Do not publish stage-specific natural-conception probabilities, EFI probability tables, a universal post-operative conception window, IVF live-birth percentages, or a personal probability verdict. Femio may explain that a clinician can use surgical findings and other factors in decision-making, without computing a score.
- Pain that occurs near an estimated fertile window remains nonspecific. Femio may overlay user-entered pain and cycle timing, but must not identify the pain as ovulation or an endometriosis flare.

## 5. Product wording

- Required footer: “Femio helps you track symptoms and prepare for a clinician conversation. It does not diagnose, stage, treat, or manage endometriosis.”
- Fertility prediction surfaces additionally say: “Predictions are estimates, not guarantees, and are not a method of birth control.”
- Ban “cure,” “reverse,” “detox,” diet-as-cure, “safe day,” “infertile day,” and any claim that a normal scan excludes endometriosis.
- Acute or severe symptoms use the project’s general urgent-care routing; Femio does not decide that a symptom is caused by endometriosis or rule out another cause.

# Changelog
All notable changes to Femio V2.

## [Unreleased]
### Changed
- **Every guide now says who wrote it and who checked it** (P1.9.8): all 30 health pages carry a named clinician with credentials — on the page, and in the structured data search engines read — linked to a bio you can open. The homepage says so too, as the first of six things you can verify. It did not say so before, and that was deliberate: the line was written, then removed, because at the time no page actually carried a byline and a promise you cannot check on the page itself is worse than no promise. The one page that gets an author and **no** medical reviewer is "Meet Femio" — it explains the product rather than your health, and a review stamp there would spend the credential where it means nothing.

- **The app looks and behaves like an app now — starting with the way you move around it and the first thing you see** (P1.28.1/P1.28.2, ADR-051): the three text links at the top have become a proper bottom tab bar with icons, where your thumb already is. Signing out moved into Settings, where it belongs, instead of sitting one mis-tap away from Track. And your home screen no longer opens with two identical-looking cards asking you to work out which one matters: it opens with the answer — the day of your cycle, when your next period is expected, and **a bar that draws that window rather than just naming it**, so a wide, uncertain estimate visibly looks wide. Under it, in plain words, is how confident that estimate is: Femio has been working this out since the beginning and had never shown it to you. There is still no countdown and still no "you are X days late", in any mode — if the window passes, Femio says so, says cycles shift, and leaves the conclusion where it belongs. Everything was checked on a phone-sized screen, in English, in Arabic right-to-left, and in dark mode.

- **Logging is built for a thumb now** (P1.28.3, ADR-052): the day you are logging for sits at the top of the Track screen as a row of days you can tap — today, yesterday, any of the last two weeks — instead of a date box buried in the middle of a form. Days you have already recorded a period on are marked. The symptom buttons are large enough to hit without looking and respond when you press them. The date field is still there for anything older, and it and the day row are always the same choice. Saving now tells you it saved.

- **The pregnancy and menopause home screens got the same treatment** (P1.28.4, ADR-053): pregnancy now shows the weeks you are, and a bar that draws **full term — 37 to 42 weeks** — with today marked inside it. The old bar filled toward your due date, which made that one date look like a finish line; most babies do not arrive on it, and the five-week range is both the truer picture and the kinder one. Menopause gets the same clear layout and, on purpose, **no bar at all**: a progress track filling toward the twelve-month mark would look like a countdown to a diagnosis, and that is a conclusion for you and your clinician, not for an app.

- **Buttons respond when you press them, and the app respects "reduce motion"** (P1.28.6, ADR-055): tapping anything in Femio used to do nothing visible until whatever it triggered finished — on a slow connection, that is indistinguishable from a broken button. Every button now answers a touch immediately. And if you have asked your phone or computer for less motion, Femio now honours it everywhere, including the pulsing placeholders that appear while your data loads. Two things that had been left behind also caught up: the symptom buttons in pregnancy mode are now the same comfortable size as the ones in cycle mode, and the life-stage picker in Settings now behaves like the one in signup.

- **Signing up looks and works like the first screen of a real app** (P1.28.5, ADR-054): the Femio name is on it now — it wasn't — along with a progress bar showing how short it is, proper radio buttons so you can see which choices are choices before you tap one, and a Continue button pinned to the bottom of the screen at full width instead of tucked in the corner after a long scroll. Screen readers now hear "option 2 of 4" instead of "button, pressed", which is what these controls actually are.

### Fixed
- **Two newly-published security advisories closed the day they appeared**: a high-severity flaw in a YAML parser and a follow-up fix in a CSS tool, both reaching Femio only through the content-management system's own dependencies. Neither was ever exploitable in the app you use, and both are now on patched versions. Femio's dependency report is back to **zero known issues** — checked at a stricter level than the build requires.

- **In pregnancy mode, the day you picked was ignored** (ADR-056): the Track screen shows a row of days at the top, but the pregnancy symptom list was not listening to it — choose "two days ago", log nausea, and it was filed under today. That screen also kept showing stale entries after saving. Both are fixed, and pregnancy logging now behaves exactly like cycle logging, which is what it always looked like it was already doing.
- **Screens shifted slightly as they finished loading** (ADR-056): the page margins were set in one place for the loaded view and a different place for the loading view, so everything nudged the moment your data arrived. One definition now, so it cannot drift again.

- **Arabic, French and Spanish were missing for pregnancy and menopause entirely** (P1.28.4, ADR-053): every word on those screens — 122 phrases, including the pregnancy warning-signs box and the symptom list — had been left in English. In Arabic that was worse than untranslated: English text inside a right-to-left page gets reordered, so a due date read as "2026/11/Due date: 06" and a week count as "weeks, 0 days 27". All three languages are now written properly. The safety wording is a first draft awaiting a human reviewer, exactly as the crisis-help wording is, and a new check now fails the build if a whole section is ever left in English again.

- **Anything you logged appeared to vanish** (ADR-052): after saving a symptom or a period, the screen kept showing what it had shown before — "no symptoms logged yet" under a symptom you had just recorded, and a calendar missing the period you had just entered. Nothing was ever lost; the page simply was not re-reading your record, and a reload would show everything correctly. It has been that way since the tracker screens were rebuilt for phones, and it was invisible from inside the code: it was found by driving a real browser through the flow. Your entries now appear the moment they are saved.

- **The "Need help now?" button was hidden behind the new tab bar** (ADR-051): found by looking at a real phone screen rather than a desktop browser, and fixed before it shipped. The crisis-help button now sits clear of the bar on every signed-in screen, in both reading directions. Nothing that leads to urgent help is ever allowed to be the thing that gets covered.

### Added
- **You can now change how Femio is set up for you, whenever your life changes** (P1.27.1, ADR-050): Settings has a new "Your Femio experience" section. Pick cycle, trying to conceive, pregnancy, or perimenopause/menopause — or choose **General**, which assumes nothing about your stage and simply gives you everything. Until now the question was asked once, during signup, and there was no way back: a wrong answer was permanent, and a real change — becoming pregnant, no longer being pregnant, periods stopping — had no way of reaching the app. **Changing it never deletes anything.** Your record stays whole, every tool stays available, and switching back finds it exactly as you left it. Femio's promise is one record across a whole life, and this is the control that makes that true rather than merely stated.
- **A movement log for pregnancy — and deliberately not a "count to ten" kick counter** (P2.2, ADR-049): you can time a session and tap each time you feel your baby move. What Femio does with it is show you *your own* pattern: how far apart movements were this time compared with your recent sessions. What it will never do is tell you a number is enough. The guidance Femio publishes — and follows — is that there is no normal number of movements for everyone, and that what matters is a change from your baby's usual pattern. A counter that congratulates you for reaching ten can reassure exactly the person who should be phoning, so this one has no target and no finish line. The line telling you to contact your maternity team if movements are less than usual sits **above** the counter and stays there the whole time: it never asks you to finish counting first. You can stop a session at any point and it still saves, because stopping to make a phone call is the most important thing this could record. Works offline, is included in your data export, and is deleted with your account.

### Changed
- **The tracker screens load faster, and they can now become real phone apps** (P4.0, ADR-048): every screen you use when signed in — your dashboard, logging, settings, the visit summary — was being rebuilt by a server on each visit. They are now delivered as a ready-made page that fetches your data straight from the database in your browser. Two things follow. The pages arrive quicker, because the server no longer has to assemble them first. And, more importantly, the same screens can now be packaged into the iOS and Android apps: a phone app has no server behind it, so the old design could never have shipped to a store — something nobody had discovered because nothing had tried to build for a phone yet. Nothing about what the screens show has changed. One visible addition: if your data cannot be loaded, you now get a clear message and a retry button instead of a page that looks empty — because "you have logged nothing" and "we could not reach your records" are very different things to tell someone about her own health record.
- **Every page got about a third lighter** (P1.11.2, ADR-037): Femio was sending the *entire* set of translations to your browser on every page — the privacy policy, the terms, the two-step-sign-in copy and the staff dashboard, on a page showing none of them. Now only the text a page can actually use is sent. A guide page dropped from 62 KB to 40 KB, and Arabic — which costs more bytes per character — from a larger figure to 46 KB. That is a real difference on a slow phone or a metered connection, and pages that load faster also rank better, which matters for a site people find through search. A check now runs on every change so it cannot quietly creep back.

### Added
- **The first 30 health guides are in the CMS, waiting for a clinician** (P1.9.4): 25 articles and 5 condition pages were imported from the drafts, each carrying its sources, its search title and description, and the disclaimer type the page needs. Every one arrived as a **draft** — nothing is public, and nothing can be until a clinician reviews and approves it. The one article that was already live was left untouched, deliberately: once a reviewer owns a page, an automated import does not get to overwrite it.
- **A PMDD guide that names the risk it should** (P1.9.3): the premenstrual guide now says plainly that low mood in PMDD can include thoughts of suicide, citing the NHS, and points to the urgent-help box on the same page. It was already safe — it had the red-flag box and a link to the crisis page — but it had not said the thing itself, and for a condition where this is a defining feature, not saying it is its own kind of omission. As everywhere in Femio, there is no phone number: the crisis routing is global, because a wrong or out-of-date number is a safety failure.

### Fixed
- **The content CMS was returning a server error, and the cause was a question nobody answered** (ADR-030): the guides API had been down since the publishing-fields deploy, because the database change that deploy needed had never actually run. The migration tool looked like it was hanging — five deploys had each burned Vercel's 45-minute build limit, and it behaved the same way locally, so it had been ruled a broken tool and taken out of the build. It was not hanging. It prints a yes/no safety question and waits for someone to type an answer, and in an automated build there is nobody there; 45 minutes was the build system giving up, not the tool running. Answered, it finishes in **under two seconds**. The API is back to normal, and there is now a documented command that answers the question so this cannot happen again.
- Closed the last known vulnerable dependencies (P1.23.2, ADR-022): upgrading the translation library clears an **open-redirect** flaw — the kind used to bounce someone from a trusted address to a malicious one — plus a second issue. Femio's dependency report is now at **zero known advisories**, from fifteen (one critical) at the start of this security pass.
- **Unknown article URLs returned a server error instead of "page not found"** (P1.2.2 / ADR-021): any stale, mistyped or renamed link under `/learn`, `/conditions` or `/authors` produced a 500 in production — bad for readers and worse for search engines, which log it as a site fault rather than a missing page. It had been live for weeks and was invisible in local development, which renders pages differently. Fixed, and Femio now has a proper localized 404 page in all four languages (right-to-left in Arabic) that offers a way back to the homepage or the library.
- The claims-language guard could report a false pass: run from anywhere but the repo root it found no message catalogs, scanned nothing, and still printed its ✓. It now fails loudly instead — this is the check that keeps Femio's copy out of medical-device scope, so silence had to mean "clean", not "didn't look".
- CMS publishing was impossible — no admin account existed (ADR-018, → apps/cms/scripts/set-user-roles.mjs): the content CMS had a single staff account holding only the `editor` role, and roles can only be changed by an admin, so nobody could approve or publish content or manage users (Payload's "create first user" screen skips the admin-only rule but still applies the editor default). Added a small, reviewable bootstrap script — `pnpm --filter cms set-roles` — that repairs an account's roles (and can reset a lost CMS password) through Payload's own API, so hooks, validation and password hashing behave exactly as in the admin panel. One command unblocks publishing (runbook C3.7).
- Critical client-side hydration crash (all signed-in pages): `env.ts` validated the whole `process.env`, which Next.js leaves empty in the client bundle, so validation threw and `/welcome`, `/track`, `/dashboard`, and `/settings` crashed on hydration in production (SSR/curl couldn't detect it). Now references each `NEXT_PUBLIC_*` var explicitly. Caught by the new Playwright e2e harness and verified fixed on live prod.

### Changed
- Femio reads as a **health** product, not a medical one (ADR-028): "Medically reviewed" is now "Clinician reviewed", "Medical review" is "How we review", and similar clinic-flavoured wording is gone across all four languages — naming the clinician who checked a page says more than the word "medical" ever did. What did **not** change is the boundary: "not medical advice", "not a medical device" and "seek urgent medical care" stay word for word, because those sentences are the protection rather than the decoration. And the boundary now appears **before** you read a guide — a short line under the byline — instead of only at the end.

### Added
- **A content guard that reads every article draft against the authoring brief** (P1.9.1, ADR-031, → `scripts/content-lint.mjs`): checking sixty health articles by hand is slow and gets less careful as it goes — and the checks that matter most are exactly the mechanical ones attention fails at: a missing top-tier source, an invented reviewer name, a page title that collides with another page. One command now covers all of it — front-matter completeness, drafts-only status, claim language wherever *Femio* is the actor, at least two sources with one top-tier and no blogs or Wikipedia, uniqueness of every title and description across the whole set, internal links resolved against the site's **real routes** rather than a list someone has to remember to update, the required shape of a page (a direct answer first, a "When to see a doctor" section, a red-flag box wherever a symptom can be an emergency), health-not-medical wording, crisis-safety routing, and a ban on phone numbers. It deliberately does **not** judge medicine: whether a claim is true and complete stays with the clinician, and the guard exists so nothing mechanical wastes that reviewer's time. A separate self-test hands each rule an article built to break it and fails if the rule stays silent — a guard nobody has watched fail is indistinguishable from no guard — and it immediately caught a real bug in the guard itself.
- **A way to get articles into the CMS without losing half of them** (P1.9.2, ADR-031, → `apps/cms/scripts/import-articles.mjs`): until now the only route was a person pasting articles into the admin panel one at a time, which silently drops the fields that make a health page trustworthy — its sources, its search description, its disclaimer type, its author and reviewer. The importer carries all of that across, and refuses anything it cannot carry faithfully rather than creating a page that looks complete and is not. Everything arrives as a **draft**: publication stays a clinician's decision behind the review gate, and the importer has no way to override it.
- Articles can now carry a real byline (P1.6.6): the CMS gained public author and medical-reviewer fields linked to the bio pages, plus dedicated search and social fields (title, description, canonical, social image) and a first-published date. Published guides show who wrote them and which clinician reviewed them, with credentials and links to their profiles — and search engines get the same in structured data. Editors can write a page's search title and description separately from its headline.
- Error monitoring that can't see your health data (P1.4.2, ADR-026): Femio now reports crashes to Sentry so we find faults before you report them — but the reports are cut down first. The page address is replaced by its *shape* (`/[locale]/learn/[slug]`), so we know which page broke without learning which article you were reading; and anything you had just typed, your cookies and your identity are removed entirely. Session recording is off. Reports go to EU servers only — a non-EU address fails the build on purpose.
- Product analytics now actually reports — for people who opt in (P1.20.1/P1.20.4, ADR-025/ADR-027): the PostHog connection is live behind the consent toggle. Two things are worth knowing about how it is built. The analytics code is **not even downloaded** until someone turns the toggle on — not loaded-and-quiet, genuinely not fetched. And the page address is stripped from every event before it is sent: a URL like `/learn/endometriosis-symptoms` says something about your health, so it never leaves your browser, along with referrers and campaign tags. What is sent is a coarse area name (“library”, “calculators”) and nothing else — each page states its own area as a fixed label, so there is no code anywhere that could turn a web address into an analytics event. Sign-up and sign-in are counted by method and outcome only, never with your life stage, email or identity; calculator use is counted once per visit and never says which calculator, because “due date” and “fertile window” would each imply something about your health.
- A proper error page when something breaks (P1.2.4, ADR-024): if a page fails unexpectedly, Femio now shows a branded, translated screen — in all four languages, right-to-left in Arabic — that says your logged data is safe and offers a retry or a way home, instead of the framework's bare error text.
- Two-step sign-in (P1.23.3, ADR-023, → Settings → "Two-step sign-in"): you can now protect your account with a code from an authenticator app on top of your password — scan a QR code, confirm one code, done. It's **required for staff accounts**: anyone with back-office access must pass the second step before the translation portal or the insights dashboard will open, so a stolen staff password isn't enough on its own. Available in all four languages.
- Security hardening of the site itself (P1.23.1, ADR-021): every page now ships a strict set of browser security headers — a Content-Security-Policy whose connection allow-list is built from the services Femio actually uses (so a page cannot talk to an unlisted third party at all), a ban on embedding Femio in another site's frame, HSTS, a referrer policy that never leaks which article you were reading, and a permissions policy switching off camera, microphone, location and payments. Dependency security is now enforced in CI: known-vulnerable packages fail the build, the report went from 15 advisories (one critical) to 3, and the one remaining accepted issue is written down with a reason and a review date. Dependabot raises grouped weekly updates.
- Product insights dashboard for staff, computed first-party (P1.20.3, ADR-020, → apps/web/src/app/[locale]/(app)/admin/insights/): an admin-only page answering how Femio is used — life-stage focus, persona, age band, language, account type, and retention by signup month. The numbers are aggregated **inside our own database** and never sent to an outside analytics company, because on a real person those dimensions are health inferences. Nothing but labels and counts ever leaves the database, any group smaller than 10 people is withheld, and small groups are merged until what remains cannot be worked out by subtraction — so a cell can never point at an individual. Needs migration 0006 (runbook A3); until then the page explains that rather than erroring.
- Manage your privacy choices in Settings (P1.20.2, ADR-019, → apps/web/src/components/account/consent-settings.tsx): a new **Your privacy choices** card lets you turn each optional permission on or off whenever you like — showing you patterns from your entries, contributing anonymized data to research, and (new) anonymous product analytics. Until now these were fixed at sign-up with no way to change your mind. Core storage consent is shown too, with a plain explanation that it is what makes Femio work and that deleting your account is how you withdraw it. Onboarding now also offers the analytics choice, off by default. Analytics never includes anything you log — no symptoms, dates, cycles or conditions — and never the crisis-help screen.
- Author & medical-reviewer bio pages (P1.9b.3b, ADR-016, → apps/cms/src/collections/People.ts, apps/web/src/app/[locale]/(public)/authors/): a **People** collection in the CMS holds bios — name, credentials, job title, affiliation, a short biography, and links to professional profiles — and the website publishes them at `/authors` and `/authors/{name}`, with `Person` structured data so search engines can see who writes and who approves Femio's health content (an E-E-A-T requirement in health). Bios are public only when published; until the first ones exist the index shows a friendly "bios appear here as our first guides are published" state. Linked from the footer, cross-linked to the editorial-policy and medical-review pages, in the sitemap, all four languages. The tables are live and verified (`/api/people` → 200); article bylines linking to these pages land with the first P1.9 articles.
- Privacy policy + Terms of use pages, published as clearly-marked **drafts** (P1.9b.3, ADR-015, → apps/web/src/app/[locale]/(public)/{privacy,terms}/): plain-language pages explaining what Femio collects and why, who else can see it (nobody but our database/hosting provider — never advertisers or third-party analytics), where it lives (EU), how long we keep it, your export/delete rights, anonymous mode, teen safeguards, and security — plus the terms, including the wellness boundary (Femio informs and tracks; it doesn't give medical advice, and fertile-window dates are not a reliable way to avoid pregnancy). Both carry a banner saying the text is a draft while our lawyers and data-protection officer finalise the binding version, and both are deliberately kept **out of search and the sitemap** until then, so a draft is never mistaken for our policy of record. Linked from the footer and cross-linked; all four languages, accessibility-checked in LTR and RTL.
- Content-first homepage + full content library + global nav (P1.9b / P1.8, → apps/web/src/app/[locale]/(public)/): the placeholder home is now a real landing page — a hero with the product promise and a card grid into the public library (Learn, Conditions, Calculators, FAQ, Glossary), with the tracker app as a portal you enter from it. Added **Conditions** guides, an **FAQ** page (with FAQ rich-results markup) and a **Glossary** (with glossary markup), all read from the CMS and reachable without an account. A **persistent site header and footer** now wrap every public page so you can move around the whole library from anywhere. Added **About**, **Editorial policy**, and **Medical review** trust pages (linked in the footer) that explain our sourcing and clinician-review standards. Full i18n; accessibility-checked.

### Fixed
- Array-valued UI messages (e.g. a page's list of points) were being collapsed into objects by the translation DB-overlay once any approved `ui_strings` existed, which would break pages that render those lists. The overlay now reconstructs arrays correctly (covered by a test).
- Public content pages from the CMS (P1.6.2 / P1.11.1, → apps/web/src/lib/cms/, app/[locale]/learn/): the web app now reads **published** articles from the Payload CMS and renders them at a `/learn` hub and `/learn/[slug]` pages — with the rich-text body, cited sources, a context-appropriate wellness disclaimer, a "medically reviewed" mark, and MedicalWebPage/Breadcrumb structured data; published articles are added to the sitemap. It degrades gracefully: if the CMS isn't configured or is unreachable, the pages show a friendly empty state and the site still builds. Full i18n. (Activate by pointing `NEXT_PUBLIC_CMS_URL` at the CMS and publishing an article.)
- Sign-in for returning users — password, magic link, and Google (P1.12.1, → apps/web/src/app/[locale]/sign-in/, reset-password/, app/api/auth/callback/): returning users (and staff who need the translation portal) can sign in with an **email + password**, a **passwordless magic link**, or **Google** (shown once you configure the provider). Forgot-password sends a reset link to a set-a-new-password page, and Settings gained a card to set/change a password (handy for accounts created passwordlessly). A callback route establishes the session and returns you to the dashboard. Full i18n.
- Bulk CSV export/import for the translation portal (P1.19.2, → apps/web/src/lib/i18n/csv.ts, components/admin/translation-editor.tsx): translators can **Export CSV** for a language (key, English master, current translation), edit all of it offline in Excel, and **Import CSV** to bulk-save — with a toggle to approve the imported rows (publish) or keep them as drafts. Unknown keys are ignored and empty cells skipped. The file uses UTF-8 so Arabic and accents open correctly in Excel.
- Translation portal + DB-backed UI strings (P1.19, ADR-014, → apps/web/src/lib/i18n/, components/admin/translation-editor.tsx, app/[locale]/(app)/admin/translations/): a staff-only portal (admin/translator) to review and approve interface strings per language, with a per-locale status board, filter, and one-click Save & approve that publishes the string immediately. Under the hood the local JSON catalogs stay the base and only **human-approved** rows from the database are overlaid on top, so nothing changes until a string is deliberately approved — an empty or unavailable table simply leaves the app on JSON (no regression). A seed script imports the JSON catalogs into the database (English pre-approved as master; Arabic/French/Spanish as drafts awaiting review). Activation needs a one-time staff-role grant + running the seed with a service key. Machine-draft prefill (DeepL) and live preview are tracked as a follow-up.
- Doctor-visit summary / PDF export (P1.16.1, → apps/web/src/lib/account/visit-summary.ts, app/[locale]/(app)/summary/): a printable `/summary` page (linked from Settings) presents a curated, human-readable snapshot — cycle count/average/range and recent periods, recent symptoms with intensity, medications, self-reported flagged topics, and key dates — for sharing with a clinician. PDF is produced by the browser's own print / Save-as-PDF, so health data never leaves your device or session (no PDF library, no server rendering); app chrome is hidden when printing. It carries a clear "personal tracking summary, not a medical record or clinical advice" disclaimer. Built on a pure, unit-tested summary builder. Full i18n.
- Per-symptom severity + custom symptoms (P1.14.1, → apps/web/src/lib/cycle/symptoms.ts, components/cycle/log-symptoms.tsx): symptom logging now records an intensity **per symptom** rather than one shared value, and lets people **add their own** symptoms (normalized + de-duplicated, stored on the open symptom type and shown as typed). The curated list grew from 16 to 26 (mood swings, low motivation, joint pain, constipation, diarrhoea, hot flushes, spotting, brain fog, higher/lower sex drive) in all four locales. The full 100+ taxonomy stays a translation-portal (CMS) task. Backed by pure helpers with unit tests and an end-to-end check.
- Fertile window + ovulation on the tracker calendar (P1.13.2, → apps/web/src/components/cycle/cycle-calendar.tsx): the month calendar now overlays the estimated fertile window (soft teal band) and ovulation day (highlighted), derived from the last period and average cycle, with a legend, screen-reader labels, and a clear conception-awareness-only note (never framed as contraception). The predicted-period band stays amber so the phases read as distinct.
- Cycle calendar + flow intensity (P1.13.1, → apps/web/src/lib/cycle/calendar.ts, components/cycle/cycle-calendar.tsx): the tracker now shows a real month calendar — an accessible table with previous/next navigation and locale-aware month/weekday labels — where logged period days are marked (with a dot whose shade reflects flow, and the flow named in each cell's screen-reader label so it never depends on colour alone), the predicted next-period window shows as a soft band, and today is ringed. Logging a period now offers a flow choice (spotting/light/medium/heavy) saved with the entry. Arabic weeks start on Saturday; RTL verified. Built on a pure, tested month-grid helper.
- Role-based access control foundation (P1.18, → docs/18_RBAC_MODEL.md, apps/web/src/lib/auth/): the web app gained role-awareness on top of the existing DB + CMS role model — `STAFF_ROLES`, pure `hasRole`/`isStaff` predicates, `getMyRoles()` (RLS-scoped, empty for consumers), and a `requireStaff()` Server-Component guard ready for the admin-portal pages (P1.19–P1.21). The RLS audit now also proves **no privilege escalation** (a non-admin cannot grant itself a role or read another user's roles). The five-role model (admin/editor/medical_reviewer/translator/support) and where each layer enforces it is documented; `support` is intentionally app-side only (no Payload CMS access).
- Security review + automated RLS audit (P1.23, → docs/compliance/SECURITY_REVIEW.md, scripts/security/rls-audit.mjs): `pnpm rls-audit` proves the data-layer guarantees against the live project using only the publishable key — the consent gate (health writes blocked pre-consent, allowed after), owner-only isolation (a second user cannot read/update/delete another's rows), the unauthenticated anon role reading no health data, and the GDPR delete cascade (10/10 pass). A SECURITY_REVIEW doc maps Femio's posture to the OWASP Top-10 (2021), documents the rate-limiting/abuse posture, and lists the remaining launch-gate items (independent pen-test, at-rest-encryption confirmation, `pnpm audit` in CI, CSP/headers, staff MFA, breach alerting).
- Accessibility audit in CI + DB-free e2e (P1.22 / P1.5.2, → apps/web/e2e/a11y.spec.ts, ci.yml): a Playwright + axe-core WCAG 2.1 A/AA scan (serious+critical gate) over home, calculators, help-now, welcome and the open crisis dialog — in both an LTR and an RTL locale — plus the crisis and home e2e now run in CI on every PR (built app served via `next start`). ### Added
- Consent-gated, health-field-free analytics foundation (P1.20, ADR-017, → apps/web/src/lib/analytics/, scripts/analytics-lint.mjs, docs/19_ANALYTICS_EVENT_SCHEMA.md): the groundwork for product analytics that **cannot** carry health data. Rather than trusting careful coding, the event catalogue is a *closed* list — fixed events, fixed properties, and every value drawn from a short fixed set, with no free-form text, numbers or dates anywhere in it — so an article slug, a symptom name, a cycle date or a lab value has nowhere to travel. A runtime check drops anything that deviates, and a new CI guard (`pnpm analytics-lint`) fails the build if the schema later drifts, if session recording or autocapture is switched on, or if anything tries to measure the crisis-help surface. Analytics is **opt-in** under its own new consent purpose and is off for everyone until the toggle ships; it is not bundled into the existing "show me my patterns" consent, which is about your own data being shown back to you. If the analytics service is ever connected it must be its EU region — a non-EU address fails the build rather than quietly sending data abroad. **Nothing is sent today**: with no analytics service configured, no third-party script loads at all.

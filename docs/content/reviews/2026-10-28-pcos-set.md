# Clinician review: PCOS cluster set (2026-10-28)

**What this is.** A source-check of four drafts that a Haiku writer produced. The review was done by an AI acting as a reproductive-endocrinology reviewer (Claude Opus 5.5). It is **not** a clinician sign-off. The front matter on all four files stays `status: draft`, with `written_by` and `medically_reviewed_by` set to `PENDING` and all dates set to `null`.

**How it was checked.** Every claim was compared with a page that was opened during this review. The 2023 International PCOS Guideline (Teede et al., *Hum Reprod* 2023;38(9):1655-1679) was read in full text on PMC (PMC10477934, the same article as the OUP URL). Recommendation numbers were checked one by one. The WHO, NHS, NIDDK, MedlinePlus and ASRM pages were opened on 2026-10-28, and their review dates are recorded in each `sources` title.

**Reading level.** A scripted Flesch-Kincaid estimate was used, with headings excluded. When run on the reviewed article `pcos-signs.md`, the script gives 6.68, against the 7.11 recorded for that article. So the script may read up to about 0.4 grades low. All four drafts stay at grade 8 or below even with that margin.

**Gate.** `pnpm content-lint` passes: 121 drafts conform. `pnpm claims-lint` and `pnpm crisis-lint` also pass. `--check-urls` was **not run**. Every URL was opened by hand instead.

**Writer's notes, resolved.**
- The guideline section numbers were wrong in places. Each one has been checked against the full text.
- All 14 `[NEEDS SOURCE]` flags are resolved: each one now has a source, or the claim was cut. None are left.
- The ACOG and NICE CKS fetches failed. Neither source is needed now: ASRM 2026 and the NHS cover the same points.
- The NHS ectopic pregnancy page is past its review date. It is kept, and a current MedlinePlus page is cited beside it.

## 1. `pcos-lifestyle-and-weight.md` (about 956 words, FK about 6.5, disclaimer: general)

**Changed**
- Lifestyle aims now follow guideline recs 3.1.1, 3.1.2 and 3.1.5. Goals are set together with the person (3.1.4). The `[NEEDS SOURCE]` definition of "metabolic health" was replaced with the guideline's own examples (blood sugar, blood fats).
- The diet section now follows 3.3.1 to 3.3.4: no diet is best, changes are tailored to food likes, and money and culture can be barriers. A warning about misinformation was added (2.6.1.5).
- The activity section now follows 3.4.1 to 3.4.4: no type of exercise is best, some activity is better than none, and less sitting helps. It gives the adult minutes plus muscle work on two days that are not back to back, and 60 minutes a day for teens. These are population activity figures, not weight or calorie targets.
- Sleep apnoea now follows 1.10.1 to 1.10.3. Snoring *combined with* waking unrefreshed, daytime sleepiness or fatigue is the trigger. Only a formal sleep study confirms it. The definition comes from the NHS.
- The weight-stigma section now follows 3.6.1 to 3.6.4. "Weight-centric" care is described as aiming for weight loss. Asking permission before weighing is given as an example. Clinician weight bias is mentioned.
- The eating-disorder wording follows 2.5.1 and 2.5.2. A short "planning a pregnancy" pointer was added (5.1.2), with a link to `pcos-and-fertility`.
- The unused NHS PCOS source was dropped. The NHS sleep apnoea page was added.

**Left out:** no weight, BMI or calorie numbers anywhere, on purpose. Weight-loss medicines are left out here; they are in the treatment article.

**Verdict:** content-accurate against the sources. I would sign it as reviewer. A human clinician should still check that the tone suits the Saudi and Egyptian audience.

## 2. `pcos-insulin-resistance.md` (about 1,019 words, FK about 6.6, disclaimer: emergency)

**Changed**
- The NIDDK source moved from the dictionary entry to the "Insulin Resistance and Prediabetes" page (last reviewed March 2025). That page backs four points: the definition, PCOS as a risk factor, "usually no symptoms", and that the insulin test is mainly a research test.
- The WHO wording is now exact: higher long-term risk of insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes, plus further blood tests after diagnosis.
- The test details now cite the guideline: OGTT 1.9.9; fasting glucose or HbA1c with reduced accuracy 1.9.10; insulin tests not for routine care 1.9.12; OGTT offered when planning pregnancy or fertility treatment 4.10.7. How an OGTT is done, fasting glucose and HbA1c are defined from MedlinePlus. Three `[NEEDS SOURCE]` flags are resolved.
- The metformin section now follows 4.3.1 and 4.3.3: metabolic outcomes, with limited evidence. It notes that the guideline prefers the combined pill over metformin for extra hair (4.4.1). Side effects come from the NHS (common effects, B12) and the guideline (4.3.4: stomach effects are dose-dependent and self-limiting).
- **The red-flag box was rewritten.** It now gives the NHS metformin page's warning signs of lactic acidosis (being sick, stomach pain, muscle cramps, breathing problems, feeling very tired) and of anaphylaxis (swollen throat or tongue, raised itchy rash, trouble breathing). It routes to local emergency services; the NHS says to call 999. The box was moved near the top, as the brief requires.
- The high blood sugar symptoms now come from the NHS type 2 diabetes symptoms page, which resolves that flag.
- A short lifestyle paragraph was added (NIDDK, guideline 3.1.1).

**Left out**
- Metformin dose and titration (4.3.4, 5.7.5.2), on purpose.
- The BMI 25 split in 4.3.1 and 4.3.3, on purpose, because it invites comparison.
- How often to repeat glucose checks: not verified, so it became a question to ask the clinician.
- A claim that dark skin patches mean insulin resistance: not stated by the sources, so it was cut.

**Verdict:** I would sign it as reviewer. A human clinician should confirm that placing a metformin emergency box on an explainer page is the right call. My recommendation is yes: the box names a real emergency route, and the wording follows the NHS leaflet.

## 3. `pcos-treatment-options-to-discuss.md` (about 1,171 words, FK about 7.4, disclaimer: emergency)

**Changed**
- Fertility options are now ordered as the guideline orders them:
  - letrozole first-line (5.3.1), and off-label in many countries (5.3.2);
  - letrozole rather than clomifene (5.4.5.1), with clomifene plus metformin also first-line;
  - clomifene preferred over metformin alone (5.4.2.1); the writer's "more effective medicines exist" line was unverified and replaced;
  - gonadotrophins (5.5.5) and laparoscopic ovarian surgery (5.6.1) as second-line;
  - IVF as third-line (5.7.0.1), with single embryo transfer (5.7.0.2).
- Pregnancy is ruled out before ovulation induction (section 5 general principles). Folate, with no dose, is part of pregnancy planning (5.1.2). OGTT before fertility treatment (4.10.7).
- The gonadotrophin definition is now from the NHS infertility treatment page. "Injected" was cut because the page fetched does not say it. That NHS page reached its review date on 9 August 2026, which is noted in its `sources` title.
- The IVF definition is from the NHS. Ovarian drilling is from the NHS PCOS page.
- Hair and acne:
  - the combined pill is preferred over metformin for hair (4.4.1);
  - anti-androgens after at least six months of the pill *and/or cosmetic therapy*, with effective contraception (4.6.1, 4.6.3);
  - spironolactone is now credited to the NHS (the guideline was not verified as naming it);
  - laser, with more sessions possibly needed (4.8.1, 4.8.2);
  - acne options from the NHS acne page, which resolves that flag.
- New paragraph: weight medicines may be considered alongside lifestyle, with effective contraception, because safety data in pregnancy are lacking (4.5.1, 4.5.2). No drug names and no targets.
- "When to see a doctor" now cites ASRM 2026: start testing without delay for irregular cycles or missing periods. This resolves the `[NEEDS SOURCE]` on time trying. It also uses the approved 3- and 6-month amenorrhea wording from `MENSTRUAL_SAFETY_THRESHOLDS.md` (ASRM 2024).
- Cut: the unsourced "very heavy periods" trigger, and the "refer to endocrinologist / blood tests" claim. A body link to `when-to-seek-fertility-help` was added; it was already declared in `internal_links`.
- **The ectopic box is kept.** The page covers trying to conceive and ovulation medicines, so an early-pregnancy emergency is in scope. The wording follows the NHS 999/A&E list. That NHS page passed its review date in August 2025, so the current MedlinePlus ectopic page (17 March 2026) is cited beside it.

**Left out / gaps**
- OHSS (ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome, a reaction to fertility medicines) and its warning signs: not sourced in this pass. A clinician should decide whether it belongs here.
- Shoulder-tip pain appears as a rupture sign on MedlinePlus and in another Femio article's box, but not in the NHS emergency list. It was left out to keep the NHS wording. A clinician should decide.
- Doses, regimens and named weight medicines, on purpose.

**Verdict:** accurate against the sources. A human clinician must check two things before I would call it signable:
1. whether letrozole is available or licensed for this use in Egypt and Saudi Arabia;
2. whether to add OHSS.

## 4. `pcos-hair-skin-and-mood.md` (about 951 words, FK about 5.8, disclaimer: emergency)

**Changed**
- The hirsutism definition and causes come from the NHS hirsutism page. PCOS is the most common cause; medicines and other hormone conditions are rarer causes.
- Scoring is described without the numbers: the cut-off varies by ethnicity (1.3.5, 1.3.6). Hair on its own is a stronger clue in adults (1.3.1). Acne or hair thinning on their own are weak clues (1.3.2).
- Other causes of acne come from the NHS acne page. Other causes of hair loss come from the NHS hair loss page. Two flags resolved.
- Mood:
  - screen all adults *and adolescents* for depression, and adults for anxiety (2.2.1, 2.2.2);
  - the screening interval wording (2.2.4);
  - body image (2.4.1) and eating disorders (2.5.1);
  - a new "Support that can help" section: psychological therapy offered (2.7.1), CBT for body image and eating concerns (2.7.2), with the CBT definition from the NHS.
- Fast changes now follow NHS hirsutism: get an *urgent GP appointment* for sudden new thick, dark hair, a deepening voice, or bigger muscles. Guideline 1.2.9 and 1.3.7 add that sudden or quickly worsening changes need further checks. Flag resolved. Tumours are not named, on purpose.
- The NHS acne GP triggers were added (deep or painful spots, scarring, effect on mental health).
- The crisis box is kept unchanged. It is number-free and links to `/en/help-now`.

**Left out**
- Numeric mFG cut-offs.
- Psychosexual function (2.3), left out to keep the conservative register.
- Adolescent PCOS criteria. They are not needed here, and the page does not label teens.

**Verdict:** I would sign it as reviewer. A human clinician should check that the depression and anxiety screening wording fits the screener Femio actually offers.

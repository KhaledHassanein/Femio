# When Should You Take a Pregnancy Test?

A home pregnancy test is often most useful from the day a period is expected. Testing too early can give a negative result even when pregnancy has begun. Follow the instructions for your test. Repeat it after several days if pregnancy is still possible.

A test result needs to be considered with timing, symptoms, and your health situation.

## How does a home pregnancy test work?

Home urine tests look for human chorionic gonadotropin, often called hCG. The body begins making this hormone after a pregnancy attaches in the uterus. It takes time for the level to become high enough for a test to detect.

The [FDA pregnancy-test guide](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/home-use-tests/pregnancy) explains that tests vary in how well they detect low hCG levels. Timing and correct use both affect the result.

## When is the best time to test?

Check the package because brands differ. In general, testing on or after the expected period gives hCG more time to rise. A test sold for earlier use can still miss a very early pregnancy.

If cycles are irregular, the expected period may be hard to judge. Think about the date of sex that could lead to pregnancy and follow the test instructions or advice from a pharmacist or clinician.

Femio's date estimates cannot know when ovulation or conception happened. Do not use an app's “late” label as proof of pregnancy.

## Does the time of day matter?

Some tests can be used at any time. First morning urine may contain more hCG, which can be useful when testing early. Avoid drinking a large amount of fluid just before testing because it may dilute urine.

Always follow the package directions. Check the expiry date, use a timer, and read the result only within the stated time window. A line that appears much later may not be a valid result.

## What does a positive result mean?

A positive home result usually means pregnancy when the test was used and read correctly. Contact a pregnancy-care professional to ask about next steps.

Review all medicines and supplements with a clinician or pharmacist. Do not stop prescribed medicine suddenly. Arrange care sooner if you have a long-term condition or a medicine that may need prompt review.

Read [pregnancy basics](/en/learn/pregnancy-basics). Then learn what may happen at the [first pregnancy appointment](/en/learn/first-pregnancy-appointment). Femio's [due-date calculator](/en/calculators/due-date) gives an estimate, not a firm date.

## What does a negative result mean?

A negative result means the test did not detect enough hCG at that time. It does not always rule out pregnancy. You may have tested early, used diluted urine, read the result at the wrong time, or misjudged when the period was due.

[NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/trying-for-a-baby/signs-and-symptoms-of-pregnancy/) advises waiting a few days and testing again if the result is negative but pregnancy is still suspected. Follow local advice and your product's instructions.

Contact a clinician if repeated tests are negative but the period does not come, or if symptoms worry you. Other health issues can also cause a missed period.

## What if the result is unclear?

An invalid result, no control line, or a mark that is hard to read means you should check the instructions. Use a new test if advised. A pharmacist or clinician can help.

Digital words and line tests work in different ways, but both can be affected by timing and use. A more expensive test does not remove uncertainty from very early testing.

## Can medicines affect the result?

Most common medicines do not cause a positive result. Some fertility medicines that contain hCG may affect a test for a period of time. Follow the clinic's testing plan if you have used one.

Rare health situations can also affect a result. Seek clinical advice when a result does not fit the timing or symptoms.

## What symptoms matter while waiting?

Common [early pregnancy changes](/en/learn/early-pregnancy-changes) include a missed period, sore breasts, tiredness, or nausea. You may pass urine more often. These changes have other causes and cannot confirm pregnancy.

Pain or bleeding can occur for many reasons. Do not wait for another home test if symptoms could be urgent. The [pregnancy warning-sign guide](/en/learn/pregnancy-warning-signs) explains when to get prompt help.

## When to see a doctor

Contact a clinician after a positive result to arrange care. Seek advice after repeat negative results if no period comes. Also ask about an unclear result, medicines, or a health concern.

> **Seek care now:** Get urgent medical help for severe one-sided pelvic or abdominal pain, shoulder-tip pain, fainting, severe dizziness, or heavy bleeding when pregnancy is possible. Contact local emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department, even if a home test is negative.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I test before a missed period?

Some products allow this, but an early negative result is less reliable.

### Should I use first morning urine?

It may help when testing early. Follow your product's directions.

### Can a negative result rule out pregnancy?

Not always. Repeat the test after the advised interval if pregnancy is still possible.

### Can Femio confirm pregnancy?

No. Femio organises dates and estimates; it cannot confirm pregnancy.

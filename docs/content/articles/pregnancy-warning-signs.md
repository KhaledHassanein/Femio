# Which Pregnancy Warning Signs Need Urgent Care?

Get urgent help for severe pain, heavy bleeding, fainting, chest pain, trouble breathing, seizures, or thoughts of self-harm. A severe headache with vision changes also needs urgent care. Seek prompt advice for fever, fluid leaking, severe vomiting, major swelling, or reduced baby movement. If something feels very wrong, seek help even when it is not on a list.

Do not wait for an app, routine appointment, or online reply in an emergency.

## Seek emergency help now for these signs

Contact local emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department for:

- Trouble breathing, chest pain, or a fast heartbeat with weakness or dizziness.
- Fainting, a seizure, confusion, or difficulty staying awake.
- Severe abdominal or pelvic pain that begins suddenly or does not ease.
- Heavy vaginal bleeding.
- A severe headache that will not go away, especially with vision changes.
- Severe one-sided leg or arm pain, redness, warmth, or swelling.
- Thoughts of harming yourself or the baby, or feeling unable to stay safe.

[CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/hearher/maternal-warning-signs/index.html) advises immediate care for signs that may point to a threat to life during or after pregnancy.

## Which signs need same-day pregnancy advice?

Contact your pregnancy-care team, urgent clinic, or local maternity service promptly for:

- Any bleeding that worries you.
- Fluid leaking from the vagina.
- Fever or feeling very unwell.
- Repeated vomiting or inability to keep fluids down.
- Pain or burning when passing urine.
- New face or hand swelling.
- A headache that persists or gets worse.
- Vision changes.
- New severe itching, especially on hands or feet.
- Baby movement that is less than the usual pattern once movement is established.

Local services may direct some of these symptoms to emergency care. Follow their advice.

## What can pain or bleeding mean in early pregnancy?

Pain and bleeding have many possible causes. Light spotting does not always mean a serious problem, but the cause cannot be known from an app or symptom list.

Severe one-sided pelvic pain can be urgent when pregnancy is possible. So can shoulder-tip pain, fainting, marked dizziness, or heavy bleeding. Seek help even if a home pregnancy test was negative.

Read [common early pregnancy changes](/en/learn/early-pregnancy-changes) for context. Do not use that guide to decide severe symptoms are safe.

## Why do headache, vision changes, or swelling matter?

A mild headache can have common causes. A severe or lasting headache, blurred vision, flashing lights, or sudden swelling may point to a pregnancy complication that needs prompt assessment.

Do not rely on a home blood-pressure reading alone. If symptoms are severe, get help even when a reading seems normal or you cannot measure it.

## When does vomiting become urgent?

Nausea and vomiting can occur in pregnancy. Seek same-day advice when you cannot keep fluids down, urine becomes very dark or scarce, you feel faint or confused, or vomiting is severe.

Severe dehydration can need urgent care. Do not wait for your next routine visit.

## What should you do about reduced baby movement?

Once you know your baby's usual movement pattern, a clear reduction or stop needs prompt maternity advice. There is no single normal number for everyone. Your baby's change from usual matters.

Do not wait until the next day. A cold drink is not a substitute for advice. Do not rely on a home listening device. Contact local maternity services.

## What if you feel unsafe or may harm yourself?

Thoughts of self-harm or harming the baby need immediate support. They are a health emergency, not a personal failure.

Use Femio's [help-now page](/en/help-now) to find immediate support options. If you may act now or cannot stay safe, contact local emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department. If possible, stay with a trusted person while help is arranged.

## How to communicate the concern

Say clearly that you are pregnant or may be pregnant. Explain the symptom, when it began, whether it is worsening, and any pain, bleeding, fever, or fainting.

Bring your pregnancy notes and medicine list if they are easy to reach. Do not delay leaving to collect them.

[ACOG](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/prenatal-care) explains why care should fit your health and pregnancy risk. After urgent care, read [pregnancy basics](/en/learn/pregnancy-basics). The [first pregnancy appointment guide](/en/learn/first-pregnancy-appointment) explains routine care.

How is this safety content checked? See Femio's [medical review page](/en/medical-review). Femio cannot assess an emergency.

## When to see a doctor

Contact a pregnancy-care professional when a new symptom worries you. Seek same-day care for bleeding or fever. Repeat vomiting, fluid loss, reduced movement, or a lasting headache also need care. Use emergency services for severe symptoms or immediate danger.

> **Seek care now:** Contact local emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department for severe pain, heavy bleeding, fainting, seizures, chest pain, trouble breathing, severe headache with vision changes, or thoughts of harming yourself or the baby. Do not wait for Femio or another online service to respond.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I wait to see whether severe pain passes?

No. Sudden or severe pain needs prompt assessment.

### Does a normal home reading rule out a serious problem?

No. Seek help based on severe symptoms even if one reading seems normal.

### Should reduced movement wait until tomorrow?

No. Contact local maternity services promptly when movement is clearly less than usual.

### What if my concern is not on the list?

Seek advice when something feels wrong. No list covers every urgent situation.

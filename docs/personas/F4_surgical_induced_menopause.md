# Persona F4 — Surgical / Induced Menopause (abrupt, unchosen menopause)

> Femio persona spec. Scope guardrail: **wellness / education / tracking only.** Femio never diagnoses, treats, or prevents disease, and **never doses medication.** HRT/MHT (here often reframed as **hormone replacement to natural-menopause age**) content is **educational, not prescriptive.** Screeners (incl. MRS) are framed as *"screening — discuss with a clinician."* Where an induced menopause sits alongside a cancer diagnosis, oncology questions route to **B19** framing ("your oncology team directs your cancer care").
> Research date: 2026-07-21 · Persona band: F (menopause lifespan) · Stage: **abrupt / induced menopause** — bilateral oophorectomy (± hysterectomy), gonadotoxic chemotherapy, pelvic radiation, or GnRH-agonist suppression. **This is not the STRAW+10 transition** — there is no gradual −2/−1 staging; onset can be from one day to the next.
> Shares the treatment-induced-menopause engine with **B19 (gynae/breast cancer survivor)**; the GSM/sexual-health layer with **B12/F6**; is POI-adjacent (**ESHRE/BMS premature-ovarian-insufficiency** guidance) when it occurs young. **Never framed as the same journey as natural perimenopause (F1) or postmenopause (F2).**

## A. Persona snapshot

**Who she is.** "Rana," 38. Three weeks ago she had both ovaries removed — for her it was a **risk-reducing salpingo-oophorectomy** after a BRCA1 result and two relatives lost to ovarian cancer (the composite also covers **severe endometriosis/adenomyosis surgery, benign bilateral oophorectomy, chemo- or radiation-induced menopause, and GnRH-agonist "chemical menopause"**). She went into the operation still having periods and woke up, effectively, postmenopausal. Within days: hot flushes that soak the sheets several times a night, a body-wide ache, a fog she can't think through, a libido that vanished, and a grief that has no ceremony — she chose this to *stay alive*, so who is she to mourn it? She is Arabic-first (also reads English), lives on her phone's browser, and every menopause app she opens assumes she is 51, went through it slowly, and can take hormones freely. **None of those three things are true.** She does not need a period predictor. She needs to understand an abrupt, unchosen, higher-stakes menopause happening 13 years early — and somewhere to put the grief.

**The spectrum F4 must handle (Femio adapts within it):**

| Axis | Range |
|---|---|
| **Cause** | Bilateral oophorectomy (± hysterectomy) — benign (severe endo/adenomyosis, PID, torsion) or **risk-reducing (BRCA/Lynch)** · gonadotoxic **chemotherapy** · **pelvic radiation** · **GnRH-agonist suppression** (endometriosis, fibroids, ER+ breast-cancer ovarian suppression). |
| **Permanence** | **Permanent** (oophorectomy, most radiation) · **may be permanent or temporary** (chemo — ovarian function sometimes recovers) · **usually reversible** (GnRH agonist — menses typically return ~3–6 weeks after stopping). *Reversibility must be tracked, not assumed.* |
| **Age** | Often **younger** than natural menopause (30s–40s common; RRSO typically 35–45) → **longer exposure to estrogen deficiency → higher lifetime bone/CV/cognitive stakes** and a stronger case for replacement to natural-menopause age. |
| **Cancer context** | May be **cancer-driven** (chemo/RT, or oophorectomy for gynae cancer → **B19**) or **cancer-preventing** (BRCA RRSO) or **cancer-unrelated** (benign endo/fibroids). HRT is *straightforward-ish* for benign/BRCA cases and *complex or contraindicated* for hormone-sensitive cancers. |
| **Fertility** | May be **grieving fertility loss** (with or without prior preservation → **C15** carry-forward) · may be past childbearing but still grieving the abruptness and the loss of choice. |

**What she lives with.**
- **Abrupt menopausal symptoms can occur** after bilateral oophorectomy because ovarian hormone production changes immediately rather than across a natural transition. Symptom type and severity vary; Femio does not publish hormone-drop or surgical-versus-natural severity percentages. [NICE NG23, updated 15 April 2026](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23/chapter/Recommendations) supplies the bounded menopause symptom and management framework. *(Reviewed Mohamed K., 24 July 2026; next review 24 July 2027.)*
- **The full symptom cluster, arriving at once** — sleep disruption, mood swings/new anxiety, "brain fog," joint/muscle aches, palpitations, and **GSM** (vaginal dryness, dyspareunia, urinary symptoms) — with none of the years-long runway a natural transition gives.
- **Higher long-term stakes** — because she is younger, untreated estrogen deficiency carries elevated risk of **osteoporosis/fracture, cardiovascular disease, cognitive decline/parkinsonism, and mood disorders** over the decades ahead (Mayo Clinic oophorectomy-cohort findings). This is *why* replacement to natural-menopause age is commonly recommended when not contraindicated.
- **Grief, identity, and loss of choice** — mourning fertility, the body she recognized, and (for RRSO/cancer) an unchosen, sudden change tied to another diagnosis. Often invalidated ("at least you're alive / at least it's over").
- **The HRT question, loaded** — for benign or BRCA-preventive surgery, replacement to ~age 51–52 is frequently recommended (and is *replacing*, not *adding*, hormones); for hormone-sensitive cancers it may be complex or contraindicated → distress and conflicting answers.
- **GnRH reversibility limbo** — for chemical menopause, "is this temporary?" and "when will my body come back?" are the defining questions.

**Core emotional drivers.** She wants Femio to **recognize this is not natural menopause** — not a slow transition she can "prepare for," but a sudden, unchosen, higher-stakes event, often young, often tied to another diagnosis. She wants **honest education on the long-term stakes and on replacement-to-natural-age** without being sold or scared, **space for grief** that isn't dismissed by "at least," and — for chemical menopause — an honest read on **reversibility.** She does **not** want a period tracker, a "you're 20 days late" counter, or a UX that treats her like a 51-year-old easing into it.

**Jobs-to-be-done.**
1. Understand an **abrupt, induced** menopause — why it feels so sudden and severe, and how it differs from natural menopause.
2. Track the symptom cluster (34+) from day one and carry a clean summary into clinician/menopause-specialist visits.
3. Understand the **long-term bone / heart / cognitive stakes** of early estrogen loss — and the rationale for **replacement until natural-menopause age** — as education to discuss with her clinician.
4. Understand **whether hormones are an option for her** at the category level (straightforward for benign/BRCA; complex/contraindicated for some cancers → route to specialist/oncology).
5. For **GnRH / chemical menopause** — understand reversibility and track whether/when function returns.
6. Grieve — fertility, body, the loss of choice — without being told "at least you're alive."
7. Keep her whole record (incl. any C15 preservation history, any B19 cancer context) in one private account, forever.

**Success = she walks into her menopause-specialist or oncology visit with a symptom timeline, her MRS trend, and the *right* questions for an induced, early, higher-stakes menopause — and she feels her grief was met, not managed — instead of fighting an app that keeps asking when her last period was.**

**Anti-needs / trust-breakers (churn drivers):**
- **Being told this is "just menopause" / the same as natural menopause.** The abruptness, the age, and the stakes are the whole point; erasing them repeats what her GP did.
- **A period/cycle/fertile-window UX** on a body that (for oophorectomy/hysterectomy) has no cycle — or a "you're late" counter.
- **HRT framed as straightforwardly available** (dangerous for cancer-driven cases) **or** as universally dangerous (wrong and cruel for benign/BRCA cases where it *replaces* what was lost). The honest answer is cause-dependent and specialist-led.
- **"At least you're alive" toxic positivity, or doom.**
- **Silence on grief, fertility loss, and sexual after-effects** — the exact gaps she came for.
- **Privacy creep** on a BRCA/cancer/sexual-health flag (post-Flo/Premom distrust).

**Feature requests recurring in reviews/forums:**
- A tracker that **starts at an event, not a cycle** (surgery date / treatment date / GnRH start), and **never** predicts a period.
- An **induced-menopause module** that names the abruptness and the *early-menopause* stakes honestly, with **replacement-to-natural-age** rationale as education.
- **Cause-aware HRT education** — benign/BRCA (replacement, generally recommended if no contraindication) vs cancer-driven (complex/contraindicated → specialist/oncology).
- **GnRH reversibility tracking** — start date, expected duration, "is it coming back" support.
- A **grief / identity / fertility-loss** support layer that never says "at least."
- **GSM / sexual-health** module (shared with B12/F6).
- **Ironclad privacy** for the BRCA/cancer flag.

## D. Clinical grounding (cited)

> All claims below are **patient-education framing**, to be medically reviewed and cited to primary sources at publish. Femio does **not** diagnose, recommend or dose any therapy (incl. HRT/testosterone), or tell any individual that hormones are or aren't safe *for her* — it explains, in plain language, what induced menopause is and routes decisions to *"your clinician (or, for cancer, your oncology team) decides with you."*

**What surgical / induced menopause is, and how it differs from natural menopause:**
- **Surgical menopause** = menopause caused by **bilateral oophorectomy** (removal of both ovaries) rather than natural ovarian aging; onset is **immediate.** Hysterectomy *alone* (uterus only, ovaries retained) does **not** cause immediate menopause, though it removes periods and may bring menopause somewhat earlier. [NCI Dictionary; Canadian Cancer Society]
- **Induced menopause** more broadly includes menopause from **gonadotoxic chemotherapy, pelvic radiation, and GnRH-agonist ovarian suppression.** Surgical and radiation-induced menopause are usually **permanent**; **chemo-induced** menopause may be permanent or temporary (function sometimes recovers, age-dependent); **GnRH-agonist** suppression is usually **reversible.** [Canadian Cancer Society; Australasian Menopause Society]
- **Abruptness and severity:** bilateral oophorectomy causes immediate surgical menopause, but individual symptoms vary. The public persona describes possible vasomotor, sleep, mood, sexual, and genitourinary symptoms without a hormone-drop percentage or a surgical-versus-natural severity ratio. [NICE NG23, updated 15 April 2026](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23/chapter/Recommendations). *(Reviewed Mohamed K., 24 July 2026; next review 24 July 2027.)*

**GnRH-agonist ("chemical" / reversible) menopause:**
- GnRH-agonist treatment can suppress ovarian function and may be used for different gynecologic or oncology indications. Recovery after stopping, the role of add-back therapy, and bone monitoring depend on the medicine, indication, duration, and person. Femio gives no return-of-menses interval, recovery guarantee, regimen, or dose; the prescriber owns these instructions.

**Long-term stakes of early estrogen loss (why replacement is commonly advised):**
- Earlier loss of ovarian function can change long-term bone and cardiovascular risk assessment, but observational cohort associations are not presented as individual predictions or treatment effects. Femio prompts a clinician discussion about bone, cardiovascular, and psychological health; it publishes no Mayo-cohort effect size or unqualified causal list. [NICE NG23](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23/chapter/Recommendations); [ASRM/ESHRE POI guideline, 2024](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/evidence-based-guideline-premature-ovarian-insufficiency--2024/). *(Reviewed Mohamed K., 24 July 2026; next review 24 July 2027.)*
- **POI parallel, with a strict scope boundary:** for clinician-diagnosed POI, [NICE NG23, updated 15 April 2026](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23/chapter/Recommendations) says to offer HRT or a combined hormonal contraceptive unless contraindicated and to continue hormonal treatment until at least the age of natural menopause. It identifies bone protection as a benefit and notes that HRT is not contraception. Femio does **not** extend this into a cognitive- or cardiovascular-prevention claim, a universal breast-cancer-safety claim, a dose, or an individualized recommendation. Treatment after cancer or cancer-risk surgery is routed to the oncology/menopause specialist. [ASRM/ESHRE POI guideline, 2024](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/evidence-based-guideline-premature-ovarian-insufficiency--2024/) supports individualized assessment. *(Reviewed Mohamed K., 24 July 2026; next review 24 July 2027.)*

**HRT / MHT after induced menopause (EDUCATIONAL only — cause-dependent):**
- For clinician-diagnosed POI, NICE recommends offering HRT or a combined hormonal contraceptive unless contraindicated and continuing treatment until at least the age of natural menopause. Femio uses that phrase rather than a fixed age and gives no route or dose. Cancer history and other contraindications require specialist assessment. [NICE NG23, updated 15 April 2026](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23/chapter/Recommendations).
- **The answer is cause-dependent:** Femio never infers that systemic HRT is appropriate from a benign-surgery or BRCA/RRSO label. It routes all decisions to the treating clinician and routes a personal cancer history to oncology/menopause expertise. [NICE NG23](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23/chapter/Recommendations) recommends specialist referral where HRT is contraindicated or the best option is uncertain; [NICE NG101](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng101/chapter/Recommendations) says not to routinely offer HRT to people with menopausal symptoms and a history of breast cancer. No “safe for you,” dose, or universal eligibility statement appears. *(Reviewed Mohamed K., 24 July 2026; next review 24 July 2027.)*
- **Testosterone / GSM:** the ~50% testosterone drop after oophorectomy and the abrupt GSM burden are commonly discussed; **testosterone for low sexual desire** and **vaginal (local) estrogen / non-hormonal GSM options** are category-level education routed to a specialist (vaginal-estrogen safety is individualized/controversial in cancer survivors — see B19). [Lancet 2023; Menopause Society GSM statement]

**MRS (Menopause Rating Scale):** the same validated 11-item, 3-domain (somato-vegetative / psychological / urogenital), 0–44 self-report used in F1 applies here as a **screening/tracking** instrument (higher = more severe), never a diagnosis. Useful as an at-onset **baseline** given how abruptly symptoms arrive. [Heinemann et al.]

**Psychological and identity support:** POI/early induced menopause can affect psychological wellbeing, fertility goals, sexual wellbeing, and quality of life. [NICE NG23, updated 15 April 2026](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23/chapter/Recommendations) recommends referral to appropriate healthcare professionals for psychological support when needed, and the [ASRM/ESHRE POI guideline, 2024](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/evidence-based-guideline-premature-ovarian-insufficiency--2024/) recommends personalized psychological support. Femio validates distress without asserting a universal grief response or attributing a symptom to menopause; mood risk or crisis signals route to clinical/local support. *(Reviewed Mohamed K., 24 July 2026; next review 24 July 2027.)*

*Femio applies all of this as **education, tracking, organization, honest framing, and emotional support**, always routing therapy and cancer questions to the appropriate clinician — never as a diagnosis, a dose, an HRT recommendation, or a "safe for you" verdict.*

**Resolved publication boundary:** quantitative surgical-versus-natural severity, hormone-drop, cohort-effect, return-of-menses, and breast-cancer-safety claims are excluded. POI wording is pinned to [NICE NG23, updated 15 April 2026](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23/chapter/Recommendations) and the [ASRM/ESHRE POI guideline, 2024](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/evidence-based-guideline-premature-ovarian-insufficiency--2024/). Cancer-related systemic or vaginal hormone questions are specialist decisions; breast-cancer GSM education follows [ACOG Clinical Consensus No. 2, December 2021](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/clinical-consensus/articles/2021/12/treatment-of-urogenital-symptoms-in-individuals-with-a-history-of-estrogen-dependent-breast-cancer). *(Reviewed Mohamed K., 24 July 2026; next review 24 July 2027.)*

## E. Feature set (prioritized)

**P0 — must ship for this persona to switch:**
1. **Induced-menopause mode — event-anchored, never cycle-anchored.** Set at onboarding from a **cause event** (surgery date / chemo or radiation start / GnRH start), **not** a last-period date. **No period prediction, no fertile window, no "late" counter, no cycle assumption** (for oophorectomy/hysterectomy the cycle is archived, never surfaced live).
2. **34+ symptom tracker with an at-onset baseline.** Full menopause symptom set (VMS, sleep, mood, cognitive, GSM/dryness, joint/muscle, palpitations, libido) logged from **day one**, viewable together over time — built for symptoms that all arrive at once.
3. **MRS screener + score** — periodic, with domain sub-scores and trend; framed as screening → "discuss with a clinician." Baseline at onset.
4. **Induced-menopause education (cause-aware) — the honest, *not-the-same-as-natural* module.** Why it's abrupt/severe; how it differs from natural menopause; the **early-menopause long-term stakes** (bone/CV/cognition) and the **replacement-to-natural-age rationale** — all education, all routed to a clinician.
5. **Cause-aware HRT/replacement education.** Benign/BRCA (replacement generally appropriate if no contraindication, specialist-led) vs **cancer-driven** (complex/contraindicated → **route to oncology/menopause specialist / B19**). Never "safe for you," never a dose.
6. **GnRH reversibility tracker (P0 for that sub-cohort).** Log GnRH start, planned duration, injection cadence; educate on expected **return of function after stopping**; support the "is this temporary / when do I come back" question; add-back is education only.

8. **Clinician visit-prep pack** — exportable timeline: symptom trends + MRS + cause/onset context + questions calibrated to *induced/early* menopause (stakes, replacement-to-natural-age, GSM, testosterone) — routes cancer questions to oncology.
9. **Privacy-by-design for a BRCA/cancer flag** — ultra-sensitive handling (see §K).

**P1 — strong differentiators:**
10. **GSM & sexual-health module (B12/F6 layer)** — non-hormonal first-line (lubricants, moisturizers), dilator/pelvic-floor education (esp. post-radiation), "how to raise it" scripts; vaginal-estrogen routed to specialist.

12. **HRT/replacement tracking log (educational)** — if clinician-prescribed, log type/route/dose + symptom response for visits; never suggests starting/changing.
13. **C15 oncofertility carry-forward** — storage vault + consent-renewal if she preserved; grief-aware fold-in if she didn't.
14. **CBT-based programs** for VMS/sleep/mood (NICE/NAMS-grounded), calibrated to abrupt onset.

**P2 — depth / retention:**
15. Partner/support-person mode (consent-scoped) — intimacy/menopause are relational.
16. Moderated, privacy-safe induced-menopause community (guarded against medical advice + doom/false-hope; young-woman-specific).
17. Stigma-aware AR/MENA content (BRCA/cancer secrecy, sexual-health taboo — "discuss privately/locally").

**Explicitly OUT of scope (guardrail):**
- Diagnosing, treating, or preventing anything; **any oncology function** (→ B19); **recommending/dosing/ruling-in-or-out** HRT, testosterone, vaginal estrogen, or add-back — category education only, always routed.
- Assuming or predicting a cycle; any "safe/fertile day" framing.
- Telling her a therapy is or isn't safe *for her*, or that her menopause is/isn't reversible as a verdict (educate on likelihoods; her clinician confirms).

## F. Content modules (referenced, medically-reviewed, grade 6–8, 4 languages, RTL-first)

Each card: source citations, medical-review byline + date, *"not medical advice — discuss options, including hormone therapy, with your clinician; for cancer, your oncology team decides with you."* Tone: honest, un-minimizing, grief-holding; **never** "same as natural menopause," **never** "at least."

1. **"This is not natural menopause — and that matters"** — why induced menopause is abrupt, often young, and higher-stakes; naming the difference her GP may have missed.
2. **"Why it feels so sudden"** — the overnight hormone drop after oophorectomy/chemo/radiation vs the years-long natural decline.
3. **"Surgical, chemo, radiation, or GnRH — what caused yours"** — cause-by-cause explainer incl. **permanence vs reversibility.**
4. **"Chemical menopause and coming back"** — GnRH agonists, expected return of function after stopping, what add-back is (education) — for the reversible sub-cohort.
5. **"The long game: bones, heart, and brain after early estrogen loss"** — why younger loss raises lifetime stakes; "know your risks, discuss DEXA/lipids/BP screening." No prevention claims.
6. **"Replacement until natural-menopause age — the honest rationale"** — why replacement (not "adding") to ~51–52 is commonly advised for early/induced menopause when not contraindicated; the *different* risk–benefit vs HRT at a normal age. Education → clinician.
7. **"Hormones and my cause — the complicated version"** — benign/BRCA (generally appropriate, specialist-led) vs **cancer-driven** (complex/contraindicated → oncology/menopause specialist; → B19). Never "safe for you."
8. **"BRCA, risk-reducing surgery, and menopause"** — the trade she made, the stakes, and that grieving a life-saving choice is allowed.
9. **"Testosterone, desire, and GSM after surgery"** — the abrupt libido/GSM burden; category-level options routed to a specialist.
10. **"GSM: dryness, pain, and sex — first things to try"** — non-hormonal first-line; dilators post-radiation; pelvic-floor; "how to raise it." (B12/F6)
11. **"Grieving what you didn't choose"** — fertility loss, body change, loss of choice; why "at least you're alive" doesn't help; that grief is valid.
12. **"You're 30-something in a 50-something's world"** — being unseen by a system built for older women; how to be taken seriously.
13. **"Getting the most from your menopause/oncology visit"** — prep, what to bring (your Femio pack), the *induced/early* questions to ask.
14. **"Fertility after treatment — grief, and what you preserved"** — folds in **C15** if present; holds loss with dignity.
15. **Myths vs evidence** — "surgical menopause is just menopause," "HRT is always dangerous," "you're too young for menopause," "it's reversible for everyone."
16. **Stigma-aware module (AR/MENA-first)** — BRCA/cancer and sexual-health privacy; secrecy-respecting; "discuss privately/locally."
17. **Reachable-but-hidden library** — full-lifespan women's-health content stays searchable; never boxed into "menopause only."

## G. Tools, logs & alerts (compliant framing)

**Logs (inputs):**
- **Cause/onset flag (sensitive):** cause (bilateral oophorectomy ± hysterectomy / chemo / radiation / GnRH agonist), sub-context (benign / **BRCA risk-reducing** / **cancer-driven → B19**), **onset date** (surgery/treatment/GnRH start), **permanence** (permanent / possibly temporary / reversible) — **entry-only, no interpretation.**
- **34+ symptom tracker** — VMS (freq/severity), night sweats, sleep, mood (anxiety/irritability/low mood), cognitive/brain fog, joint/muscle, palpitations, GSM (dryness/dyspareunia/urinary), libido, weight/body — all toggleable, viewable together, from an **at-onset baseline.**
- **GnRH tracker (sub-cohort):** start date, planned duration, injection cadence, stop date, symptom/return-of-function notes.
- **HRT/replacement log (educational):** if clinician-prescribed — type (estrogen ± progestogen ± testosterone), route (transdermal/oral/vaginal), start/stop dates, symptom response. Bring-to-clinician artifact; **no recommendation/dose/interpretation.**
- **Sexual-health log (optional, private):** dryness, dyspareunia, desire, dilator/moisturizer use.
- **Screener history:** MRS (scores + domains + dates); mood/anxiety (screening-only).
- **C15 fertility-preservation ref** (if present); or `fertility_loss_flag`.
- **Emotional tags** (post-surgical raw / grieving / reversibility-limbo) — drive support-card selection.

**Tools:**
- **Symptom-trend view** + **one-tap visit-prep export** (symptom timeline + MRS + cause/onset + calibrated questions).
- **Induced-menopause education hub** (cause-aware).
- **GnRH reversibility tracker.**
- **MRS screener** → "screening, not a diagnosis — discuss with a clinician."
- **GSM/sexual-health module** (education + optional private log + scripts).
- **Emotional-support hub** (grief/identity/fertility loss) + mood screener → screening-framed + localized crisis resources.

**Alerts (opt-in, gentle, dismissible, RTL-aware, non-alarming):**
- **No period/cycle/fertile-window nudges — ever** (for oophorectomy/hysterectomy there is no cycle).
- **GnRH milestone (sub-cohort):** *"You noted your GnRH course around [date/duration you entered] — want info on what to expect if/when it ends?"* (education).
- **Replacement-review nudge (educational):** if on clinician-prescribed replacement → annual-review framing ("prepare for your review"). Never "start/stop."
- **GSM/symptom check-in:** *"How have the flushes / dryness been this week? Logging helps your next visit."*
- **Grief-aware:** suppress all celebratory/milestone framing; scanxiety/anniversary date-control; crisis resources on low mood screens.
- **Never:** contraception/"safe day" claims; disease-prevention/treatment claims; "start/stop HRT/testosterone"; a "safe for you" verdict; a reversibility verdict presented as certainty.

## H. EMR / data fields (core vs persona-specific)

**Core (shared spine):** `user_id · dob/age_band · locale · rtl_pref · consent_flags · account_mode` · `cycle{}` (**archived/inactive** post-oophorectomy/hysterectomy — retained, never surfaced live) · `symptoms[] · mood[] · meds_supplements[] · conditions_flagged[] (non-diagnostic)` · `content_history[] · screener_results[] (timestamped, non-diagnostic)` · privacy/state flags.

**F4 persona-specific:**
- `induced_menopause_context` { cause: oophorectomy | oophorectomy_hysterectomy | chemo | radiation | gnrh_agonist ; sub_context: benign | brca_risk_reducing | cancer_driven ; onset_date ; permanence: permanent | possibly_temporary | reversible ; **elevated sensitivity for BRCA/cancer** } — **entry-only, no interpretation.**
- `gnrh_course` { start_date, planned_duration, cadence, stop_date, return_of_function_note } — reversibility tracking (sub-cohort).
- `menopause_symptoms[]` { type, severity, date } — 34+ set incl. GSM, from at-onset baseline.
- `mrs_records[]` { item scores, 3 domain sub-scores, total, date } — trend.
- `replacement_log[]` { on_therapy y/n, type (estrogen ± progestogen ± testosterone), route, dose, start/stop, symptom_response } — **educational entry only, never interpreted as recommendation.**
- `sexual_health_log[]` { dryness, dyspareunia, desire, dilator_use, moisturizer_lubricant_use, date } — optional, private.
- `long_term_health_context` (optional, user-entered): family history, prior DEXA/lipids/BP — entry only, no diagnosis.
- `fertility_preservation_ref` → **C15** storage record + consent-renewal, OR `fertility_loss_flag` (support layer).
- `emotional_phase_tags[]` (post_surgical_raw / grieving / reversibility_limbo).
- `oncology_ref` → **B19** context if cancer-driven (cancer_type/phase/treatments carried, entry-only).
- **No live cycle prediction; `ttc_intent` not assumed; contraception framing off.**

*All health fields optional, encrypted, user-deletable; the BRCA/cancer flag and sexual-health fields get the strongest handling; the replacement log is educational tracking, not medication management.*

## I. Personalization rules (wizard → home config; hidden-but-reachable)

**Onboarding wizard (branch into F4 — direct via Q6 "surgical/induced," routed from **F1** if surgical/induced signals appear, from **B19** if cancer-driven, or "my situation changed"):**
1. Menopause entry (Q6): symptoms · cycles stopped? · **"Did surgery, cancer treatment, or medication bring on your menopause?"** → **yes → F4.**
2. **Cause capture:** "What brought it on?" (oophorectomy ± hysterectomy / chemotherapy / radiation / a medication like a GnRH injection) ⇒ sets `cause`, `onset_date` anchor, and **permanence** default.
3. **Sub-context (sensitive, opt-in):** benign (e.g., endometriosis/fibroids) · **risk-reducing (BRCA/family risk)** · **because of / alongside a cancer** ⇒ if cancer-driven, **offer B19 overlay** and cancer-aware HRT framing; sets privacy sensitivity.
4. **(If GnRH)** "Is this expected to be temporary?" ⇒ activate **reversibility tracker.**
5. Top symptoms (multi-select) ⇒ order home cards/content.
6. "Are you on hormone therapy / replacement?" → yes ⇒ surface educational replacement log; no ⇒ cause-aware replacement education (never push; for cancer-driven, route to specialist).
7. **(If fertility)** "Did you preserve fertility before treatment?" → yes ⇒ carry **C15**; no/unsure ⇒ hold gently, fold into support.
8. Consent & privacy (explicit ultra-sensitivity notice for BRCA/cancer flag).

**Home configuration (default for F4):**
- **Hero = onset-anchored symptom snapshot + "this is induced menopause" orientation** — *no cycle card, no next-period date, no fertile window, no "late" counter.*
- **MRS card** (baseline + trend) pinned.
- **Induced-menopause education** + **long-term-stakes / replacement-rationale** pinned (cause-aware).
- **Grief/identity support** surfaced early (raw post-onset window).
- **GnRH reversibility card** pinned for that sub-cohort.
- **GSM/sexual-health** surfaced if flagged.
- **Cancer-driven → B19 overlay** (cancer-aware HRT framing, surveillance held by B19).
- **Suppress/hide:** cycle prediction, fertile-window, period/pregnancy/TTC nudges (reachable via search, never pushed).

**Adaptive tone:** un-minimizing, grief-holding, age-aware ("you're younger than the system assumes; the stakes and the care are different"). **Never** "same as natural menopause," **never** "at least you're alive," **never** toxic positivity or doom. Rising MRS psychological sub-score ⇒ surface mood/CBT + crisis resources. On-replacement + symptom relief ⇒ "prepare for your review."

**Hidden-but-reachable:** all lifespan content (natural menopause, fertility, pregnancy) stays discoverable via search / "Explore other life stages" — foregrounded differently, never deleted.

All routes **preserve data continuity (one account)**, require **explicit user confirmation**, and route therapy/cancer questions to the appropriate clinician — never a dose, an HRT "safe-for-you" verdict, or a reversibility certainty.

## K. Compliance flags

1. **Scope:** wellness / education / tracking ONLY. No diagnosis, treatment, disease-prevention, or contraception; **no cycle assumption** post-oophorectomy/hysterectomy. (Aligns with Femio compliance-privacy skill + medical-device scope avoidance.)
2. **Never "same as natural menopause."** The abruptness, younger age, higher long-term stakes, and cause-dependent HRT picture are clinically material; content and tone must reflect the difference (echoing NICE/ESHRE/BMS POI framing) and must not present an induced menopause as an ordinary transition.
3. **HRT/replacement = educational only, cause-dependent.** Explain what replacement is, the **replacement-to-natural-age** rationale, and the *different* risk–benefit for early/induced menopause; **never** recommend, initiate, dose, or advise stopping/changing; **never** say hormones are or aren't "safe for you." For **cancer-driven** cases, HRT may be complex/contraindicated → **route to oncology/menopause specialist (B19)**. The replacement log is a bring-to-clinician artifact, not medication management. [NAMS 2022; ESHRE/BMS POI; BGCS–BMS 2024]
4. **Testosterone / vaginal estrogen / add-back:** category-level education only; routed to specialist; vaginal-estrogen safety individualized/controversial in cancer survivors (→ B19). Never dosed.
5. **GnRH reversibility = education, not a verdict.** Educate on *likely* return of function after stopping; never guarantee reversibility or its timing for an individual — "your clinician confirms."
6. **Long-term health:** framed as “ask your clinician about your bone, cardiovascular, and psychological health”; no prevention, treatment, causal, or individualized-risk claim and no cohort effect size. [NICE NG23](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23/chapter/Recommendations); [ASRM/ESHRE POI guideline, 2024](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/evidence-based-guideline-premature-ovarian-insufficiency--2024/). *(Reviewed Mohamed K., 24 July 2026; next review 24 July 2027.)*
7. **MRS & all screeners:** always "This is a screening tool, not a diagnosis — discuss with a clinician." Never "You are in menopause / You have depression."
8. **Emotional safety paramount (high-acuity, unchosen loss).** Tone avoids **both doom and toxic positivity**; **never** "at least you're alive/it's over"; grief over fertility, body, and the **loss of choice** validated; celebratory/cycle/milestone framing suppressed; crisis resources on any low mood screen; silence-over-harm on grief paths.
9. **Ultra-sensitive data (BRCA, cancer, treatment, and sexual-health fields).** Never send these data to ad networks, use them for targeted advertising, or expose them to employers/insurers. Apply minimum collection, granular/revocable product permissions, purpose-limited processors, and strict retention, export, deletion, and security controls under [`HEALTH_APP_CLAIMS_AND_DATA_SAFETY.md`](../compliance/HEALTH_APP_CLAIMS_AND_DATA_SAFETY.md). Primary warnings: [FTC Flo](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/192-3133-flo-health-inc) and [FTC Premom](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/202-3186-easy-healthcare-corporation-us-v). *(Reviewed Mohamed K., 24 July 2026; next review 24 July 2027.)*
10. **GDPR / PDPL — special-category health data.** Lawful basis + explicit consent; DSAR/export/delete; regional data-residency (esp. AR/MENA); DPIA before launch; extra scrutiny for the BRCA/cancer flag.
11. **Not contraception; no cycle assumption.** Retained cycle history archived, never surfaced as a live prediction; never "safe/fertile day"; **no "you're late" state, ever.**
12. **C15 carry-forward & fertility loss.** Preservation storage records (incl. consent-renewal) persist unchanged; fertility loss held with grief-aware, non-blaming UX.
13. **Medical review + citations.** Public clinical content is bounded by [NICE NG23, updated 15 April 2026](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23/chapter/Recommendations), the [ASRM/ESHRE POI guideline, 2024](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/evidence-based-guideline-premature-ovarian-insufficiency--2024/), [NICE NG101](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng101/chapter/Recommendations), and [ACOG Clinical Consensus No. 2, December 2021](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/clinical-consensus/articles/2021/12/treatment-of-urogenital-symptoms-in-individuals-with-a-history-of-estrogen-dependent-breast-cancer). Excluded quantitative claims cannot re-enter through localization without a new reviewed tracker entry. *(Reviewed Mohamed K., 24 July 2026; next review 24 July 2027.)*
14. **Stigma-aware localization (AR/MENA-first).** BRCA/cancer and sexual-health matters treated as private; secrecy-respecting, maximum-privacy defaults; sensitive content framed as *discuss privately/locally.*
15. **App-store health-app policy.** Apply [`HEALTH_APP_CLAIMS_AND_DATA_SAFETY.md`](../compliance/HEALTH_APP_CLAIMS_AND_DATA_SAFETY.md): no HRT recommendation, reversibility promise, diagnosis, dosing, or treatment selection; substantiate sexual-health and menopause claims, provide required disclosures/clinician prompts, and complete Google’s health-app declaration. Re-check both store policies and obtain launch-market legal review immediately before submission. *(Reviewed Mohamed K., 24 July 2026; next review 24 July 2027.)*

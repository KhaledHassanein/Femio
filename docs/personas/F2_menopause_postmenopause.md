## A. Persona snapshot

**Who she is.** "Salma," 55. Her last period was two and a half years ago, so the cycle chaos that defined her mid-forties is over — no more flooding, no more "is this the last one?" But she is *not* "done." She still wakes at 3am soaked in sweat several nights a week, her joints ache, sex has become painful and she has stopped mentioning it, and she has started to wonder — quietly — whether her bones and her heart are quietly changing while she isn't looking. Her mother broke a hip at 68. She has moved on from "am I in perimenopause?" to "what do I do about the next 30 years?" Arabic-first (also reads English), web-first on her phone, private, and tired of apps that either treat her like a fertility patient or assume her story ended the day her periods stopped.

**What she lives with (the postmenopause cluster).**
- **Persistent vasomotor symptoms (VMS)** — hot flashes and night sweats that did **not** stop at menopause; for many women they last years into postmenopause (SWAN: median total VMS duration ~7.4 years, ~4.5 years *after* the final period; longer for some groups). The core surprise F2 must validate: "why am I still getting these?"
- **Genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM)** — vaginal dryness, irritation, painful sex, urinary urgency/recurrent UTIs. Unlike VMS, GSM is **progressive and does not resolve on its own** — it tends to worsen with time postmenopause. Hugely under-reported. → routes to **F6**.
- **Bone health / osteoporosis risk** — accelerated bone loss in the years around and after menopause; awareness + "discuss screening" is the job, never a prevention claim.
- **Cardiovascular risk** — CVD is the leading cause of death in women, and risk rises postmenopause; lifestyle/awareness framing.
- **Weight & metabolic changes** — central/visceral fat gain, muscle loss, shifting lipids/glucose.
- **Sleep** — fragmentation and wakefulness that persist and feed daytime fatigue and mood.
- **Mood & cognition** — processing-speed dips, word-finding; usually stabilizes, not a decline story.
- Plus: joint aches, palpitations, skin/hair changes, libido.

**Core emotional drivers.** She wants **to be seen as still here** — a whole woman with three decades ahead, not a "post-reproductive" afterthought. She wants **honest long-term-health literacy** (bones, heart, brain) framed as *agency and healthy aging*, not decline and dread. She wants her **still-present symptoms validated** ("no, you're not the only one still flashing at 55"). She wants **honest MHT education** — including the reality that initiation windows and the risk-benefit picture shift with age since menopause — without being pushed toward or away from hormones. And she needs to know the **one hard red line**: any bleeding after the 12-month mark is not "a last hurrah," it is a **see-a-clinician-now** sign.

**Jobs-to-be-done.**
1. Understand what postmenopause actually is and what the next decades look like — without decline framing.
2. Keep tracking persistent symptoms (VMS, sleep, mood, joints, GSM) and see them over the long term.
3. Build bone / heart / metabolic literacy and know *what screening to ask about* and *when*.
4. Get honest, referenced education on MHT continuation vs initiation windows, and non-hormonal options.
5. Know the postmenopausal-bleeding red flag cold — and act on it.
6. Feel that midlife and beyond is a stage of strength, not loss.

**Success = she treats postmenopause as a long, active health chapter — tracks what persists, walks into appointments asking the right bone/heart/GSM/MHT questions, and knows instantly that any bleeding means "call the clinician," not "wait and see."**

## D. Clinical grounding (cited)

**Menopause / postmenopause — definition & staging.**
- Natural menopause = permanent cessation of menstruation from loss of ovarian follicular activity, recognized **retrospectively after 12 consecutive months of amenorrhea** with no other cause; the years afterward are **postmenopause** [WHO fact sheet; MedlinePlus/NIH].
- **STRAW+10 (2012):** the 12-month point ends stage +1a; **stage +1 (early postmenopause)** spans roughly the first ~5–8 years (a period of ongoing FSH rise and continued symptom activity and accelerated bone loss); **stage +2 (late postmenopause)** follows, dominated by somatic aging and urogenital changes (GSM) [STRAW+10, PMC/ASRM]. F2 sits at +1 → +2.

**Persistent vasomotor symptoms.**
- VMS are **not** brief and frequently persist well into postmenopause. In **SWAN (JAMA Intern Med, 2015)**, median total duration of frequent VMS was **7.4 years**, persisting a median of **4.5 years after the final menstrual period**; women with early onset had the longest courses (median ≥11.8 years) and African American women the longest overall (~10.1 years) [SWAN/Avis et al., JAMA Intern Med 2015]. This grounds F2's central validation: still having VMS years out is common, not abnormal.

**Genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM).**
- GSM (vulvovaginal atrophy + urinary symptoms) affects a large share of postmenopausal women — estimates range widely (~13–87%; commonly cited ~50–70%) — and unlike VMS is **chronic and progressive**, tending to worsen over time. It is heavily **under-reported and undertreated** [Contemporary OB/GYN; ICSM 2024; AUA/SUFU/AUGS 2025 guideline]. Femio surfaces GSM education/tracking and routes to **F6**; management is clinician-led.

**Bone health / osteoporosis.**
- Estrogen decline accelerates bone resorption; women can lose **up to ~20% of bone density in the ~5–7 years around/after menopause**, raising osteopenia/osteoporosis and fracture risk [Cleveland Clinic; NIH/PMC].
- **Bone-density (DXA) screening:** the [USPSTF final recommendation, 14 January 2025](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/document/RecommendationStatementFinal/osteoporosis-screening) recommends screening women aged 65 years or older. For postmenopausal women younger than 65, it recommends a two-step clinical assessment: identify one or more risk factors, then use a clinical risk-assessment tool to determine increased fracture risk before screening. Its listed factors include low body weight, parental hip-fracture history, smoking, and excess alcohol use. This applies to adults without known osteoporosis, fragility fracture, or secondary osteoporosis; Femio only prompts “ask your clinician whether screening applies to you.” *(Reviewed Mohamed K., 24 July 2026; next review 24 July 2027.)*

**Cardiovascular risk.**
- Per the **AHA 2020 Scientific Statement (Menopause Transition and CVD Risk)**, CVD is the leading cause of death in women, and risk rises across the transition into postmenopause; blood pressure, total/LDL cholesterol, triglycerides, and metabolic syndrome prevalence rise around late peri / early post, alongside visceral fat and arterial stiffness. The statement emphasizes **early lifestyle-based prevention and counseling** [El Khoudary et al., Circulation 2020 / AHA]. Femio frames as "know your numbers, discuss screening/prevention with your clinician" — never a prevention claim.

**Weight, metabolism, sleep, cognition (healthy-aging framing).**
- Postmenopause is associated with **central/visceral fat gain, muscle loss, insulin-resistance shifts, and adverse lipid changes**; poor/fragmented sleep interacts with weight and mood [PMC; npj Aging]. **SWAN** cognition data: processing speed may dip, but verbal/working-memory declines typically start later and cognition largely **stabilizes** — the honest message is *change, not inevitable decline* [SWAN Cognition fact sheet, NIA-supported]. Femio's tone: positive, agency-forward healthy aging, not deficit.

**MHT / HRT (EDUCATIONAL framing only — Femio never prescribes, initiates, doses, or advises stopping).**
- Per **The Menopause Society (NAMS) 2022 Hormone Therapy Position Statement**, HT remains the **most effective treatment for VMS and for GSM**, and it prevents bone loss/fracture. The **benefit–risk profile is favorable for symptomatic women aged <60 or within 10 years of menopause onset** with no contraindications; it becomes **less favorable when initiated >10 years from onset or at age >60**, due to greater absolute risks of CHD, stroke, VTE, and dementia. Importantly for F2: **initiating HT after age 65 was associated with increased dementia risk** in the WHIMS (CEE+MPA arm), and **HT is not recommended at any age to prevent/treat cognitive decline or dementia**; early-postmenopause HT has **neutral** cognitive effects. Transdermal/low-dose routes may lower VTE/stroke risk. Continuation past 60/65 is individualized shared decision-making, not an automatic stop [NAMS 2022; WHIMS].
- Femio explains continuation vs. (re)initiation windows and the age-since-menopause risk gradient — always routing to a clinician. It is **not** a recommendation to start, stop, or continue any therapy.

**Non-hormonal options (educational).**
- Per **The Menopause Society (NAMS) 2023 Nonhormone Therapy Position Statement**, Level-I evidence supports **CBT, clinical hypnosis, SSRIs/SNRIs** (low-dose paroxetine 7.5 mg is the only FDA-approved non-hormonal for VMS; plus escitalopram, venlafaxine, desvenlafaxine), **gabapentin**, and **fezolinetant** (Veozah, NK3-antagonist, FDA-approved 2023). **NICE NG23** supports menopause-specific CBT and fezolinetant when HRT is unsuitable; the **IMS** likewise supports individualized, evidence-based non-hormonal and hormonal options across postmenopause [NAMS 2023; NICE NG23; IMS]. For **GSM specifically**, low-dose **vaginal** estrogen and non-hormonal moisturizers/lubricants are clinician-discussed options (→ F6).

**Postmenopausal bleeding (PMB) — the F2 red flag.**
- Bleeding that starts after menopause needs prompt clinician assessment; Femio does not estimate cancer probability or prescribe a test sequence. The global surface says “contact a clinician promptly” and lets the country module map the destination. For the UK only, [NICE NG12, updated 15 April 2026](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng12) supplies the age-, HRT-, and symptom-specific suspected-cancer referral rules. Other markets must use their own verified national pathway; Femio never labels the symptom as cancer. *(Reviewed Mohamed K., 24 July 2026; next review 24 July 2027.)*

*Publication rule:* osteoporosis wording is pinned to the 2025 USPSTF recommendation above. Postmenopausal-bleeding destinations are country-module content (NICE NG12 for the UK); global copy gives prompt-clinician routing only. Secondary-review prediction or app-quality figures are excluded from public claims. *(Reviewed Mohamed K., 24 July 2026; next review 24 July 2027.)*

## F. Content modules (referenced, medically-reviewed, grade 6–8, 4 languages)

1. **Postmenopause 101** — what "past 12 months" means, STRAW+10 +1/+2 in plain language, what the next years/decades typically look like — framed as a health chapter, not an ending.
2. **Why am I still having hot flashes?** — VMS persistence (SWAN durations), that years-out VMS is common, and what helps.
3. **Genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM)** — dryness, painful sex, urinary symptoms; that it's common, progressive, treatable, and worth raising — normalizing a taboo topic (→ F6 for depth).
4. **Your bones after menopause** — accelerated bone loss, osteoporosis/fracture risk, what protects bone, "ask about a DXA / bone-density scan."
5. **Your heart after menopause** — why CVD risk rises, the numbers that shift (BP, cholesterol, glucose), lifestyle that helps, "know your numbers / ask about screening."
6. **Weight, metabolism & muscle at midlife** — visceral fat, muscle loss, insulin/lipid shifts; non-shaming, strength/nutrition-forward.
7. **Sleep, mood & memory after menopause** — persistent sleep issues, mood, and honest cognition messaging (change, largely stabilizes — not inevitable decline).
8. **Understanding MHT after menopause (education only)** — continuation vs (re)initiation, the <60 / within-10-years window, the >60 / >10-years and >65 risk gradient (CHD/stroke/VTE/dementia), routes/doses, GSM-specific vaginal estrogen — plain language, non-prescription disclaimer, "discuss with your clinician."
9. **Non-hormonal options** — CBT, clinical hypnosis, SSRIs/SNRIs, gabapentin, fezolinetant, GSM moisturizers/lubricants — education only.
10. **Postmenopausal bleeding: what to do** — clear, calm, unambiguous: **any bleeding after 12 months needs a prompt clinician visit** (endometrial-cancer red flag; usually benign but always investigated). Never minimized.
11. **Healthy aging & you** — an agency-forward "next 30 years" module: strength, bone/heart habits, sexual health, cognition, purpose — anti-decline.
12. **Myths vs evidence** — "HRT causes cancer," "you're too old to start/stay on HRT," "a little bleeding is normal now," "hot flashes stop at menopause," "there's nothing to do about painful sex."

Every module: referenced citations, medical-review byline + date, "discuss with your clinician" footer, no prescriptive/dosing language for MHT.

**Tone rule (F2-specific — healthy aging, not decline):** copy is agency-forward and forward-looking ("your next chapter," "build strength," "know your numbers"), validates persistent symptoms ("still flashing years out is common — you're not alone"), and avoids both doom ("everything declines now") and toxic positivity. GSM and sexual-health copy is matter-of-fact and de-stigmatizing.

**Adaptive tuning:** rising MRS psychological sub-score ⇒ surface mood/CBT + crisis resources. Repeated GSM logs ⇒ raise F6 routing prominence. On-MHT + approaching typical review interval ⇒ "prepare for your review" (annual-review framing per NICE) + continuation-window education. Any post-FMP bleed ⇒ PMB red-flag takes priority over all else.

**Hidden-but-reachable principle:** perimenopause history, cycle/fertility education, and all other content stay discoverable via search + "Explore other life stages." Nothing is deleted; F1 history is archived-but-reachable.

## J. Adaptation triggers (life-stage routing)

**Inherited universal events** (from Master Adaptation Model §6, applied here): mood-screen-high/crisis → mental-health module + localized crisis resources; red-flag symptom/severe reading → "seek care" education box (PMB is F2's flagship instance); disengagement/"taking a break" → pause nudges, keep data; toggle-mode-off → revert to generic logic, preserve history; age/consent milestones → re-confirm privacy tier. These are **not** re-specified below.

**F2-specific transitions:**

| Trigger | System response | Route |
|---|---|---|

## K. Compliance flags

- **Scope:** wellness / education / tracking ONLY. No diagnosis, no treatment, no disease-prevention claim, not contraception. (Aligns with Femio compliance-privacy skill + medical-device scope avoidance.)
- **Postmenopausal bleeding:** **always** surfaced as "see a clinician promptly," **never normalized, minimized, or reassured away, and never diagnosed** ("this could be cancer" is not stated as a claim; "probably nothing" is not stated either). Education + urgent-clinician routing only — never triage.
- **MRS & all screeners:** always return "This is a screening tool, not a diagnosis — discuss your results with a clinician." Never output "You are postmenopausal," "You have osteoporosis," "You have heart disease," or "You have depression."
- **HRT/MHT content & log:** **educational only** — explain continuation vs initiation windows, benefits/risks by age-since-menopause (incl. >65 dementia signal), routes/doses, GSM vaginal estrogen; **never recommend, initiate, dose, continue, or advise stopping/changing.** MHT log is a *bring-to-clinician* artifact. Non-prescription disclaimer everywhere hormones appear.
- **Non-hormonal/medication content** (SSRIs/SNRIs, gabapentin, fezolinetant, GSM moisturizers/lubricants): educational context only; no recommendation/dosing; clinician routing.
- **Bone / CV / metabolic long-term health:** framed as "know your risk, know your numbers, ask about screening" — no prevention/treatment/screening-mandate claims. DXA/BP/lipid/glucose entries are the user's own records, never interpreted or scored by Femio.
- **Healthy-aging tone:** agency-forward, anti-decline, anti-doom, anti-toxic-positivity; GSM/sexual-health copy de-stigmatized and matter-of-fact.
- **Mental health:** localized crisis resources on any low mood screen or high MRS psychological sub-score.
- **Privacy (GDPR/PDPL health data):** symptom, MRS, GSM, MHT, and long-term-health data = special-category; explicit consent, purpose limitation, **no ad-SDK/third-party sharing** (post-Flo trust); local-first where feasible; full export/delete; DPIA + data-mapping before launch. GSM/sexual-health data treated with heightened sensitivity.
- **Content integrity:** all medical content referenced, medically reviewed with byline + date, YMYL/E-E-A-T compliant; grade 6–8 reading level; 4 languages with Arabic-first/RTL QA.
- **App-store health policy:** avoid medical-device trigger language; tracking/education framing keeps out of regulated-device territory — legal review before store submission.
- **Source boundary resolved:** use [USPSTF 2025](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/document/RecommendationStatementFinal/osteoporosis-screening) for US osteoporosis screening and [NICE NG12, updated 15 April 2026](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng12) only within the UK referral module. No secondary app-quality or prediction statistic is public. *(Reviewed Mohamed K., 24 July 2026; next review 24 July 2027.)*

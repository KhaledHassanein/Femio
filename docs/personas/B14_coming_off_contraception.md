**Persona thesis.** B14 is the mirror-image of C11: same biological event (**stopping contraception**), opposite intent. C11 asks *"how do I flip the switch to trying?"*; B14 asks *"what is my body actually like without these hormones, and is what I'm feeling normal?"* Her defining need is a **method-specific, honest "what to expect" map of the adjustment period** — return of periods (and the withdrawal-bleed-vs-true-period distinction), symptom changes (post-pill acne, mood, libido, cramps), and the Depo delay — plus the discipline to **distinguish normal readjustment from an underlying problem the pill may have masked** (e.g. PCOS). Thread these correctly and she trusts Femio as her body-literacy companion for years.

## B. Needs & questions (voice-of-user)

Mined from r/birthcontrol, r/stopdrinking-adjacent wellness communities, "coming off the pill" forums (Endometriosis UK / HealthUnlocked, The Lowdown, Patient.info comment threads), and app-store reviews. Phrasing is representative/composite (community norms; not quoting individuals).

**The core JTBD (post-contraception adjustment, NOT conception):**
1. *"I came off the pill to give my body a break — what actually happens now, week by week?"*
2. *"What is my natural cycle even like? I've been on hormones since I was 16 and have no idea."*
3. *"Is this withdrawal bleed my period? When does a 'real' period come back?"*
4. *"My acne came roaring back after I stopped — is that permanent? (post-pill acne)"*
5. *"My cycles are all over the place / haven't come back — is that normal or a problem?"*
6. *"It's been months off Depo and still no period — is something wrong?"* (Depo-specific distress)
7. *"Did the pill hide something? My periods were irregular before I started — are they just going back to that?"* (masked-PCOS worry)
8. *"Is 'post-birth-control syndrome' real? Do I need to detox / take these supplements everyone's selling?"*
9. *"I don't want to get pregnant right now — since I'm off hormones, what do I do?"* (contraception-need, must route to clinician + barrier education)
10. *"My mood / libido / energy changed after stopping — is that the hormones leaving, or me?"*
11. *"When should I actually see a doctor about all this?"*

**Emotional arc (this persona's distinctive shape):**
- **Decision / stop moment:** relief + curiosity + a little "finally" — an identity move ("I want to feel like myself").
- **The adjustment limbo (weeks–months):** the acute pain point — breakouts, mood swings, cramps returning, unpredictable or absent bleeding. *"Is this normal or did I break something?"* Worst for Depo (months of no period) and for anyone whose pre-pill cycle was irregular.
- **Settling:** cycle finds a rhythm (natural, possibly different from the pill's tidy 28-day bleed). She pivots to normal cycle-awareness (→ B1).
- **If it doesn't settle / a condition surfaces:** creeping worry, heavily fed by "post-birth-control syndrome" content and supplement marketing → needs calm, cited routing, not fear.

**Anti-needs / trust-breakers (what makes her churn):**
- A **"your period is late / X days late" counter** during expected post-stop recovery — reads as alarm when the app should say *"this is expected."*
- **Assuming she's TTC** — surfacing fertile-window/ovulation-for-conception nudges the moment she logs an irregular post-stop cycle. She specifically left that framing behind.
- **Endorsing "post-birth-control syndrome" as a diagnosis** or pushing detoxes/cleanses/supplement funnels — but *also* the opposite failure: dismissing her real adjustment symptoms as "all in your head."
- Treating all methods the same (telling an implant user to expect the Depo delay, or vice-versa).
- Any **"safe days / infertile day / you can't get pregnant today"** framing — doubly dangerous because she may be relying on *something* to avoid pregnancy. Femio is not contraception.
- Weight-loss / body-shaming framing around post-stop weight changes.

**Feature requests recurring in reviews/forums:**
- A **method-specific "what to expect while adjusting" timeline** ("you stopped [method] on [date] — here's the honest range and the common bumps").
- **Calm, cited myth-handling** on "post-birth-control syndrome" and detox marketing.
- A **symptom-change tracker** for the things that actually change coming off (acne, mood, libido, cramps, flow, spotting) viewable together over time.
- A gentle **"switch from pill-reminder to cycle-awareness"** flow — turn off pill alarms, turn on natural-cycle logging (without turning on TTC).
- Honesty that **the first bleed is often a withdrawal bleed, not a true period**, and that ovulation can resume before any bleed (relevant to her pregnancy-avoidance need).

## D. Clinical grounding (cited)

> All claims below are patient-education framing. Femio content is medically reviewed and cites primary sources. Femio does **not** diagnose, treat, prevent, or contracept; it educates and helps users track.

**Return of periods / cycles BY METHOD (the routing backbone — critical for §J):**
- **Combined pill / POP:** the synthetic hormones clear within days; **periods usually return within about 1 month**, though it can take **several months** to settle into a regular pattern. The first bleed after the combined pill is often a **withdrawal bleed** (hormone-withdrawal, not ovulation-driven), with the first *true* period following roughly a month later. [NHS — when periods return after stopping the pill; Cleveland Clinic]
- **IUD (hormonal or copper) & implant (Nexplanon):** natural hormone cycling resumes **quickly after removal**; expect **1–3 months** of readjustment (spotting, cramping, mood shifts) as the body re-establishes its own cycle; most cycles normalise within this window. [GoodRx — IUD removal; Nexplanon removal patient education]
- **Injection (Depo-Provera / DMPA):** the **one method with a materially delayed return.** Periods and ovulation can take **up to ~1 year (occasionally up to 18 months)** to return after the last injection, even though the injection only "lasts" ~13 weeks. This is a **delay, not damage** — Depo does not cause permanent infertility or permanent amenorrhea. **No period yet in the first ~12 months post-Depo is expected**, not a red flag. [NHS — contraceptive injection; ASRM/ACOG patient education; DMPA prescribing data]

**Withdrawal bleed vs. true period (a signature B14 teaching):**
- On the combined pill the monthly bleed is a **withdrawal bleed**, not a true menstrual period — the pill suppresses ovulation, so there is no ovulatory cycle behind it. After stopping, the **first bleed is often another withdrawal bleed**; the return of a *true, ovulatory* period may take a further few weeks. Explaining this prevents the "is this my period?" confusion. [ACOG — menstrual suppression consensus; NHS]

**Post-pill amenorrhea (the "when to check in" anchor):**
- If **no period returns within ~3 months** of stopping (and pregnancy is excluded), this is often called **post-pill / secondary amenorrhea** and is worth discussing with a clinician — usually it resolves, but it can be the cue to check for underlying causes. Reassuring context: studies show the **median time from withdrawal bleed to first menses is ~32 days (range ~15–82)**, and ~**99% of users show normal hypothalamic-pituitary function within 90 days**. The pill does **not** cause the amenorrhea; it may have **masked** a pre-existing cause. [Secondary-amenorrhea clinical references; NHS; ACOG]
- Femio applies the 3-month mark (pill/IUD/implant) and the **>12-month** mark (Depo) as **educational routing** — "worth discussing with a clinician" — **never** as a diagnosis.

**Post-pill acne & symptom changes (validate, don't pathologise):**
- Some combined pills have an **anti-androgen effect** that suppresses acne; stopping can cause acne to **return or flare within weeks–months** (one cited study reported ~63% experiencing skin blemishes after stopping). It often settles as the body readjusts. [Natural Cycles — post-birth-control acne; The Lowdown]
- Other common, generally self-limiting adjustment changes: **mood shifts, changes in libido, cramps returning (dysmenorrhea the pill had eased), heavier/longer flow, PMS/PMDD symptoms, breast tenderness, bloating, water-weight changes, spotting/breakthrough bleeding.** These typically **improve within 1–3 months** as hormones stabilise. Mood and libido changes are among the **top reasons women discontinue** hormonal contraception in the first place. [Cleveland Clinic; WebMD; Eur J Contracept Reprod Health Care 2022 (PMC9133050)]

**"Post-birth-control syndrome" — the myth to handle carefully (signature guardrail):**
- **"Post-birth-control syndrome" (PBCS) is NOT a recognised medical diagnosis** — it is not in ICD-11 and is not recognised by ACOG or ESHRE. It circulates via social media and naturopathic/commercial "hormone-detox" content, leaving users vulnerable to **misinformation and commercial exploitation**. [Frontiers/PMC12283672 — feminist-lens review of PBCS media narratives; Wiley PSRH 2025 — TikTok content analysis]
- **BUT the individual adjustment symptoms are real and validated** (acne, irregular cycles, mood changes). Femio's job is to **validate the symptoms and their timeline while declining to endorse the "syndrome" label, any "detox," or supplement funnels.** No "cleanse," no mandatory wait, no supplement prescription. [same reviews; NHS]

**The pill can mask underlying conditions (critical differentiator):**
- Hormonal contraception creates a **regular pseudo-cycle** and lowers androgens, which can **mask undiagnosed PCOS** (and other causes of irregular cycles). When someone whose cycles were **irregular before** starting the pill stops, those symptoms often **re-emerge** — this is the pill's masking effect lifting, **not** the pill "causing" PCOS. Persisting irregularity/hyperandrogenism beyond ~3–6 months is a cue to discuss screening. [Center for Young Women's Health; Texas Health Care OB/GYN; Flo — "can birth control cause PCOS?"]

**Pregnancy-avoidance while off hormones (must be handled — she is NOT TTC):**
- Stopping hormonal contraception means **fertility can return immediately** — you can conceive **before** the first bleed, because **ovulation precedes menstruation**. A woman coming off hormones who does **not** want to conceive should discuss an **alternative method** (e.g. barrier/condoms, copper IUD, or other options) with a clinician. Femio educates on this need and routes it — **Femio itself is not a contraceptive and offers no "safe days."** [NHS — planning/contraception; MedlinePlus — fertile days; Cleveland Clinic]

*Flagged for medical-review sign-off before ship:* exact Depo percentage/median figures and "up to 18 months" phrasing; the post-pill acne "63%" figure; the withdrawal-bleed median "32 days (15–82)" and "99% within 90 days" figures; pill "within a month" wording; the 3-month post-pill-amenorrhea threshold per locale. **[NEEDS SOURCE VERIFICATION AT PUBLISH]** for any figure stated without a per-locale reviewed citation. **[NEEDS SOURCE — FSRH CEU primary guidance on return of fertility/cycles by method — cite directly at publish.]**

## E. Feature set (prioritized)

Range + confidence, never a promised date, **never framed around conception.**

3. **Symptom-change tracker.** Log and view *together* over time the things that actually change coming off: acne, mood, libido, cramps, flow, spotting, energy, breast tenderness, weight (optional). Correlate with cycle phase as data builds — descriptive, not diagnostic.
4. **Calm, cited "post-birth-control syndrome" / detox handling.** Front-and-centre content that **validates real adjustment symptoms**, explains the honest timeline, and **declines to endorse** the unrecognised "syndrome," detoxes, or supplement funnels. This is the persona's signature content.
5. **"Pill-reminder → cycle-awareness" switch flow (NOT TTC).** One guided step that turns **off** pill/patch/ring reminders and turns **on** natural-cycle + symptom-change logging — **without** enabling fertile-window/TTC framing. Re-confirms privacy scope for the new data class.
6. **Pregnancy-avoidance routing card (honest, non-contraceptive).** If she indicates she wants to avoid pregnancy while off hormones: a calm card — "fertility can return right away, even before your first period; talk to a clinician about a method that fits you" + barrier-method *education*. **Explicitly not contraception, no 'safe days'.**
7. **Method-aware "when to talk to a clinician" routing card.** Surfaces at the method-specific window (~3 mo pill/IUD/implant; >12 mo Depo) if no period, or if pre-pill-irregular symptoms persist. Calm, non-diagnostic.

**P1 — Strong differentiators (fast follow):**
8. **Depo-specific companion track** — the hard case: longer, reassurance-forward timeline, "no period yet is normal here," *no* premature worry framing before ~12 months.
9. **"Was your cycle irregular *before* contraception?" capture + masked-condition awareness** — sets honest expectations that pre-existing irregularity (e.g. PCOS-adjacent) may re-emerge; if it does, soft-route to B6/screening content **without labeling**.
10. **Post-pill acne / skin module + optional private photo progress log** (local) — the most-searched specific symptom for this cohort.
11. **Natural-cycle literacy onboarding** — for users who've been on hormones since their teens and have never seen their own cycle: teaches what a natural cycle looks like, cycle-length variability, cervical-mucus/temperature *awareness* (as body literacy, **not** as contraception or TTC).

**P2 — Nice-to-have / later:**
12. Trend correlations ("your low-mood logs cluster in the week before bleeds") — pattern insight, not diagnosis.
13. Community / moderated Q&A (privacy-safe) — the post-pill cohort seeks peer reassurance and is a prime target for misinformation; a moderated, cited space is a trust asset.
14. Localized coaching by market (AR modesty-aware; contraception history is sensitive).
15. Symptom-timeline export (PDF/image) to hand a clinician if the method window elapses or symptoms persist.

**Explicitly OUT of scope for B14 (guardrail):**

## F. Content modules

Medically reviewed, referenced, grade 6–8 reading level, all four locales, RTL-first for AR. Each card carries citations and "not medical advice / discuss with your clinician."

- **"Coming off contraception: what to expect, honestly"** — the anchor card. Method-neutral overview of the adjustment period, framed for *body awareness*, not conception.
- **"When will my periods come back?" — by method** — one card per method (pill, IUD, implant, injection) with the honest return range. The Depo card is the longest and most reassuring.
- **"Withdrawal bleed vs. your real period"** — why the first bleed often isn't a true period, and what a returning ovulatory cycle looks like.
- **"Post-pill acne (and other skin changes)"** — why it happens (anti-androgen effect lifting), the typical weeks–months timeline, self-care, when to see a clinician.
- **"Mood, libido and energy after stopping"** — validating that these change, the usual settling window, when a persistent low mood warrants support (links to mental-health module).
- **"Is 'post-birth-control syndrome' real? Do I need to detox?"** — the calibrated myth card: **validates the symptoms**, explains PBCS is **not a recognised diagnosis**, and firmly says **no detox/cleanse/mandatory supplements** are needed — while pointing to what *does* help (time, tracking, clinician if persistent).
- **"Did the pill hide something? (masked conditions)"** — how the pill can mask PCOS/irregular cycles; if your periods were irregular *before*, they may return that way; how to tell readjustment from a condition worth screening.
- **"The Depo timeline, honestly"** — dedicated: why the injection is different, the up-to-~12-month (occasionally 18) range, "no period yet is expected," when to talk to a clinician.
- **"Not trying to get pregnant right now?"** — honest, non-contraceptive: fertility can return immediately (even before a bleed); talk to a clinician about a method that fits; barrier-method *education*. Explicit "Femio is not birth control."
- **"Getting to know your natural cycle"** — cycle-length variability, what's normal, body-literacy primer for people who've never tracked off-hormones.
- **Reachable-but-hidden library:** full-lifespan women's-health content — including **contraception *education*** (methods, how they work, side-effect profiles), PCOS, gynae conditions, TTC, perimenopause — stays searchable so B14 never feels boxed into "coming-off content only."

**Mandatory compliance wording on every predictive/timeline surface:**
- *"These are estimates based on your method and your data — not a guarantee, and **not a method of birth control**."*
- Screener outputs: *"This is a screening tool, not a diagnosis. Please discuss results with a clinician."*

4. "Which method did you stop?" → sets `contraception_method_stopped` (drives the whole timeline + routing).
5. "When did you stop / have it removed / have your last injection?" → sets `contraception_stop_date`.
6. "Why are you coming off it?" (multi-select) → sets `stop_reason`, orders home modules (side-effects → symptom-change tracker up; body-awareness → natural-cycle literacy up).
7. **Calibrated reassurance interstitial** — "Coming off contraception, your body readjusts over weeks to months. Some changes (like skin or mood) are common and usually settle. You don't need a detox — we'll show you the honest timeline for your method." (cited; defuses PBCS/detox fear up front.)
8. "Were your periods regular *before* you started birth control?" → sets `pre_contraception_cycle_regularity`, soft-routes masked-condition expectations.
9. "Do you want to avoid pregnancy right now?" → sets `pregnancy_avoidance_intent`; if *yes*, queue the **non-contraceptive routing card** (barrier education + clinician). Femio provides no method.
10. Consent & privacy choices (re-confirm the new cycle/symptom data class).

**Home config for B14 (two phases):**
- **Phase 1 — adjusting / pre-true-period (the B14-defining state):** Hero = **method-anchored adjustment timeline** ("you stopped [method] on [date] — here's the honest range and common changes"). Cards: symptom-change tracker, post-pill-symptom content matched to `stop_reason`, PBCS/detox myth card. Cycle UI shows **trend + "this is expected," never a late-counter**, with withdrawal-bleed explainer. If `pregnancy_avoidance_intent=yes`, pin the non-contraceptive routing card.
- **Phase 2 — settled natural cycle:** on `first_true_period` + a couple of logged cycles, **offer the B1 home** (general cycle tracking + symptom insight). This is the handoff.
- **Hidden-but-reachable:** full lifespan library, contraception *education*, condition pages, TTC, perimenopause — one search/tap away. TTC mode is opt-in only.

**Adaptive tone:** reassuring + validating + curiosity-supporting throughout Phase 1 (especially Depo and post-pill acne); shifts to standard B1 body-literacy tone in Phase 2. Never alarmist, never "your body is failing," never toxic-positive, never a supplement pitch.

**Adaptive tuning:** if pre-pill-irregular symptoms persist beyond the settling window, raise PCOS-awareness content + screening prominence (→ B6 soft-route). If mood screens trend low, surface mental-health module + resources.

## K. Compliance flags

1. **Not contraception (highest-priority guardrail).** B14 users may be **relying on nothing** to avoid pregnancy. Femio gives **no advice to stop/continue/switch a method**, offers **no "safe/infertile day"** framing, and provides **no contraceptive function**. The pregnancy-avoidance need is **routed to a clinician + barrier-method education**. Every predictive/timeline surface carries *"not a method of birth control."* Keeps Femio out of the regulated contraceptive-device lane. **[NEEDS SOURCE — confirm app-store medical-claims + health-claims wording at ship.]**
2. **No diagnosis / treatment / prevention.** The adjustment timeline and method thresholds are **educational routing/awareness**, not verdicts. "No cycle return in window" routes to a clinician; it never diagnoses amenorrhea, PCOS, or infertility. Depo delay is framed as **expected recovery, not damage.**
3. **"Post-birth-control syndrome" handled with dual accuracy.** Femio must **validate real adjustment symptoms** *and* clearly state that **PBCS is not a recognised medical diagnosis** (not in ICD-11; not recognised by ACOG/ESHRE) — while **refusing** to promote detoxes, cleanses, or supplement funnels. Cite Tier-1 + peer-reviewed media-analysis; medically reviewed; per locale. **[NEEDS SOURCE VERIFICATION AT PUBLISH]** for the PBCS-status claim and all symptom-prevalence figures.
4. **Uncertainty honesty + no "late" counter.** Post-stop recovery UI must display ranges + confidence and **must not** show "period late / overdue" during the expected window (Master invariant #5). Explains withdrawal-bleed vs true-period rather than implying lateness.
5. **Masked-condition care.** Distinguishing "readjustment" from a re-emerging condition (e.g. PCOS) must **route to screening/clinician**, never label. The pill did not *cause* the condition; it masked it — copy must say this accurately.
6. **Privacy-by-design / reproductive-data sensitivity.** Contraception history is special-category and socially sensitive (esp. AR/MENA). No sharing of cycle/contraception/symptom data with ad networks or third-party analytics (cf. FTC actions vs Flo 2021, Premom 2023). Data minimization; explicit, granular, revocable consent; strict retention; user-deletion. The "pill-reminder → cycle-awareness" switch **re-confirms** the new data-sharing scope. **[NEEDS SOURCE — FTC primary docs; align to Femio privacy skill / DPA.]**
7. **GDPR / PDPL — special-category health data.** Lawful basis + explicit consent; DSAR/export/delete; regional data-residency. **[NEEDS SOURCE — align to Femio privacy skill / DPA at implementation.]**
8. **Supplement / medication content.** Any mention of supplements or medication (incl. acne treatments, "hormone-balance" products) is **educational only** — no recommendation, dosing, or "take this," and an explicit **non-prescription / no-detox** stance. Clinician routing.
9. **Weight & body image.** Post-stop weight/water changes discussed neutrally; no weight-loss messaging; weight fields optional.
10. **Non-alarming emotional safety.** The post-stop adjustment (esp. Depo amenorrhea and post-pill acne) is anxiety-loaded and misinformation-heavy; tone must reassure without toxic positivity, validate symptoms without pathologising, and never imply a failing body or push a commercial "fix." Loss handled with grief-aware UX.
11. **Medical review + citations.** All clinical content medically reviewed, cited to primary sources, grade 6–8, 4 locales with RTL parity. Numeric claims (Depo timeline, pill "within a month," acne %, withdrawal-bleed median, 3-month rule) re-verified against live primary text at publish. **[NEEDS SOURCE VERIFICATION AT PUBLISH]**
12. **App-store health-app policy.** Meet Apple/Google health-app & medical-disclaimer requirements; no over-claiming; avoid medical-device trigger language. **[NEEDS SOURCE — map to store policies at submission.]**

## D. Clinical grounding (cited)

> All claims below are **patient-education framing**. Femio content is medically reviewed and cites primary sources. Femio does **not** direct protocols, dose or time medications, or interpret *her* results — it explains, in plain language, what an ART cycle involves and routes every specific instruction to *"your clinic directs this — confirm with them."*

**What ART / IVF / IUI is (the phase C10 is in):**
- **IUI (intrauterine insemination):** prepared sperm is placed via a thin catheter through the cervix directly into the uterus, timed around ovulation (natural or with mild stimulation/trigger). It is the **less invasive, lower-cost** first-line ART for many. [MedlinePlus/education sources]
- **IVF (in vitro fertilization) / ICSI:** eggs are retrieved, fertilized with sperm in the lab (ICSI = a single sperm injected into each egg), and resulting embryo(s) transferred to the uterus. A full IVF cycle typically runs **~4–6 weeks** from the start of stimulation to transfer. [MedlinePlus — IVF]

**The IVF cycle, step by step (education only — the clinic sets every date/dose):**
1. **Ovarian stimulation:** injectable gonadotropins (FSH ± LH) stimulate the ovaries to grow **multiple follicles**, monitored by blood tests and ultrasound. [MedlinePlus]
2. **Monitoring:** serial **transvaginal ultrasounds** (follicle number/size, endometrial lining) and **bloods** (estradiol/E2, LH, sometimes progesterone) track response and time the trigger. [MedlinePlus; clinic patient education]
3. **Trigger shot:** when follicles reach target size, a **trigger** (hCG or GnRH-agonist) matures the eggs; **egg retrieval occurs ~35–36 hours later** — *timing is exact and clinic-set.* [education sources]
4. **Egg retrieval:** a minor transvaginal-ultrasound-guided procedure (usually under sedation) aspirates follicular fluid to collect eggs. [MedlinePlus]
5. **Fertilization & embryo development:** eggs fertilized (IVF or ICSI); embryos cultured and monitored over ~3–5 days (day-5 = blastocyst). [MedlinePlus]
6. **Embryo transfer:** one (or more) embryo(s) placed into the uterus, typically **3–5 days** after retrieval (fresh), an in-office procedure while awake. [MedlinePlus]
7. **Two-week wait → beta-hCG:** a **blood pregnancy test (beta-hCG) ~2 weeks** after transfer confirms outcome; progesterone support is usually continued through the wait. [education sources]

**Protocol types (education only — the clinic assigns and adjusts these; Femio never selects, doses, or times them):**
- **GnRH-antagonist ("short") protocol:** FSH stimulation without an initial down-regulation phase; a GnRH antagonist (e.g., cetrorelix/ganirelix) is added mid-stim to prevent premature ovulation. **Now the most common protocol** — fewer injections, shorter, and **lower OHSS risk**. [ESHRE ovarian-stimulation guideline — strong recommendation for antagonist over standard long agonist for the general population]
- **GnRH-agonist ("long") protocol:** a **down-regulation** phase (e.g., leuprolide) suppresses the natural cycle before FSH stimulation; ~4–5 weeks. If an agonist protocol is used, ESHRE recommends the long over short/ultrashort variants. [ESHRE]
- **Others** referenced in education: **mini-/mild-IVF**, and **freeze-all** strategies. Choice is individualized by the clinic (age, ovarian reserve, prior response, OHSS risk).

**Frozen embryo transfer (FET) — a distinct C10 sub-flow (see §J):**
- A previously frozen embryo is thawed and transferred; the endometrium is prepared either **medicated (programmed/HRT: estrogen then progesterone, predictable date, minimal monitoring)** or **natural (tracked to the body's own ovulation)**. [HFEA; clinic education]
- **Elective "freeze-all"** — freezing all embryos and deferring transfer to a later FET — is used routinely **to reduce OHSS risk** and for other clinical reasons; HFEA treats freezing/FET as routine, well-established practice. [HFEA — Elective freeze-all cycles]

**OHSS — Ovarian Hyperstimulation Syndrome (the red-flag safety spine — see §G):**
- OHSS is an **uncommon but potentially serious complication** of ovarian stimulation, where the ovaries over-respond and fluid shifts into the abdomen (and, when severe, the chest). [ASRM/ReproductiveFacts; MedlinePlus; RCOG]
- **Mild:** bloating, mild abdominal discomfort, nausea, slight weight gain. **Moderate–severe:** rapidly worsening abdominal swelling/tightness and pain, persistent vomiting, **shortness of breath / difficulty breathing**, **reduced/dark urine**, rapid weight gain, and **calf/leg pain or swelling** (possible clot). [ASRM 2023 guideline; MedlinePlus; NHS trust patient leaflets; Mayo]
- **Timing:** can appear in the days after **egg collection**, and sometimes only once pregnancy hormones rise (later onset if the cycle worked); it can take days to weeks to resolve. [NHS trust leaflets]
- **Seek-care framing (education):** *symptoms that are clearly worsening → contact the fertility clinic promptly; breathing difficulty, severe/constant abdominal pain, calf pain/swelling, or markedly reduced urine → urgent/emergency care.* Antagonist protocols and **freeze-all** are among the clinic's OHSS-risk-reduction tools. [ASRM 2023; MedlinePlus; RCOG] *Femio surfaces these as education + "seek care now," never as triage or diagnosis.*

**Success-rate honesty by age (grounds the uncertainty-honest tone — see §I/§K):**
- IVF **live-birth rate per cycle declines markedly with age**. Representative national-dataset figures (HFEA/SART, own eggs): roughly **~50–55% under 35, ~40% at 35–37, ~26–27% at 38–40, ~13% at 41–42, and a few percent over 42.** [HFEA key facts; SART]
- **IUI** per-cycle pregnancy rates are far lower — often **~10–15% per cycle**, with cumulative odds rising over ~3 cycles and highly dependent on age and diagnosis. [clinic/peer-reviewed education]
- **Design implication:** show ranges + "per cycle started, not per transfer," never a single certain outcome; a failed cycle is common and **not a personal failure**; multiple cycles are frequently needed. [HFEA; SART]

**Emotional impact (grounds the heavy support layer):**
- Distress during IVF/ART is high and often **comparable to that of serious medical illness**; anxiety **peaks during the two-week wait** and around monitoring calls and outcomes. Duration of treatment, **financial burden**, and social support are key determinants of psychological adjustment. [ASRM/SART patient guidance; peer-reviewed IVF-distress studies]

*Femio applies all of this as **education, organization, reminders, and emotional support**, always ending *"your clinic directs your protocol — confirm every dose, date, and instruction with them"* — never as a dose, a protocol decision, a result interpretation, or a diagnosis.*

## G. Tools, logs & alerts (compliant framing)

- **Procedure log:** trigger date/time, retrieval date + eggs collected, fertilization/embryo report (fertilized, day-3/day-5, frozen), transfer date + embryo(s), beta-hCG date & value.
- **Appointments:** monitoring visits, procedure dates, which are fasting/early, ordered items, results due.
- **Cost ledger:** medication and cycle charges; per-cycle and cross-cycle totals.
- **Symptom/side-effect & mood log** (hormonal effects, injection-site reactions, TWW symptoms); mood/anxiety screener history (scores + dates, screening-only).

**Alerts / nudges (all opt-in, tone-calibrated, dismissible, revocable, RTL-aware):**

- **Emotional-arc cards:** stims-fatigue support, pre-retrieval steadying, post-transfer / TWW grounding, results-day and post-failed-cycle grief-aware support.
- **Screening follow-up** (re-check mood in N weeks) + crisis resources on any low screen.

**OHSS red-flag "seek care now" framing (safety-critical, mandatory):**
- A persistent, high-visibility safety card active from stimulation through the post-retrieval/early-TWW window:
  > **Watch for OHSS.** Some bloating and mild discomfort after your injections or egg collection can be normal. **But contact your fertility clinic promptly if your tummy swelling or pain is clearly getting worse, you're vomiting and can't keep fluids down, or you're passing much less (or very dark) urine.**
  > **Seek urgent / emergency care now if you have: shortness of breath or trouble breathing, severe or constant abdominal pain, or pain/swelling in a calf or leg.**
  > These can be signs of OHSS, an uncommon but serious complication of fertility medication. *This is general information, not a diagnosis — your clinic and emergency services decide what you need.* [ASRM/MedlinePlus/RCOG]
- Saved **clinic + emergency contact** shown as *education/quick-reach*, never as triage or a severity assessment. Femio **does not grade OHSS or tell her to stay home** — worsening or severe symptoms always route to "seek care now."

**Mandatory compliance wording on relevant surfaces:**
- *"Femio helps you organize and remember the treatment your clinic directs — it does not prescribe, dose, time, adjust, or interpret your medications, tests, or results. Only your clinic can do that."*
- On every medication/reminder surface: *"Doses and timing come from your clinic. Femio never changes them — confirm anything you're unsure of with your clinic."*
- Screener outputs: *"This is a screening tool, not a diagnosis. Please discuss results with a clinician."*
- Success/prediction surfaces: *"Estimates and ranges only — not a guarantee. Your clinic can explain your specific chances."*

6. "Where are you in this cycle?" (stims / trigger / post-retrieval / post-transfer-TWW / between cycles) ⇒ sets home emphasis, **OHSS watch**, and emotional-phase tag.
7. "Involve a partner?" → partner-mode setup (calendar view, injection-night reminders, sperm-sample/male-side notes), consent-gated.
8. Consent & privacy choices (with explicit ART-data sensitivity + secrecy/stigma notice).

## K. Compliance flags

1. **No diagnosis / treatment / prevention; no dosing; no test/result/protocol interpretation (highest-priority guardrail for C10).** Femio **mirrors and reminds** the clinic's protocol and medication schedule and **organizes** results — it never prescribes, doses, times, adjusts, selects a protocol, or interprets *her* values (E2, follicle counts, beta-hCG). Every medication and reminder surface states doses/timing come from the clinic. Medication content is **educational (category-level), never a dose.** **[NEEDS SOURCE — confirm current app-store medical-claims + medical-device scope wording at ship.]**
2. **OHSS safety layer is mandatory and must not be buried.** Clear "warning signs & when to seek care" with an explicit **"seek care now"** list (breathing difficulty, severe/constant abdominal pain, calf/leg pain-swelling, markedly reduced/dark urine); saved clinic/emergency contact as *education*. Femio **never grades OHSS severity or advises staying home** — worsening/severe → seek care. [ASRM 2023; MedlinePlus; RCOG] **[NEEDS SOURCE VERIFICATION AT PUBLISH — exact symptom list + timing per region.]**
3. **Not contraception.** Any retained fertile-window surface is conception-awareness only, carries *"not a method of birth control,"* never "safe/infertile day" language.
4. **Uncertainty honesty / no false hope or false doom.** Age-anchored success **ranges**, "per cycle started," multiple-cycles-are-common; never a guaranteed outcome and never "it will never work." [HFEA; SART] **[NEEDS SOURCE VERIFICATION AT PUBLISH — per-age live-birth + IUI per-cycle figures.]**
5. **ART-data privacy (elevated sensitivity + secrecy risk).** Protocol, medications, embryo counts, and beta-hCG are ultra-sensitive special-category health data with acute stigma/secrecy risk (esp. AR/MENA and workplaces). Learn from **Flo (FTC 2021)** and **Premom (FTC 2023)**: no sharing with ad networks/third-party analytics; data minimization; explicit, granular, revocable consent; strict retention & user-deletion for uploaded protocol sheets/photos. **[NEEDS SOURCE — FTC primary docs.]**
6. **GDPR / PDPL — special-category health data.** Lawful basis + explicit consent; DSAR/export/delete; regional data-residency (esp. AR/MENA); DPIA before launch. **[NEEDS SOURCE — align to Femio privacy skill / DPA at implementation.]**
7. **Partner mode consent.** Partner sees only granted scope (calendar, injection-night reminders, male-side notes); consent logged, scope-limited, instantly revocable.
8. **Cost tracker = logging, not advice.** No financial, insurance, or coverage *advice*; education only.
9. **Medical review + citations.** All clinical content medically reviewed, cited to Tier-1 (NIH/NHS/MedlinePlus) + Tier-2 (ASRM/ESHRE/HFEA/RCOG/SART), grade 6–8, localized in 4 languages with RTL parity.

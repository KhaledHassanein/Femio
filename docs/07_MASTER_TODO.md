- [x] **R3.3.2** Symptom ↔ cycle patterns (2026-08-22). `symptom_logs` has held real data since P1.14 and **nothing read it** beyond counting the three most frequent types, while both incumbents ship pattern spotting (`docs/26` §6). `lib/cycle/symptom-patterns.ts` is tier-2 with 9 tests. The claim is deliberately small — *"cramps, on days 1–3 of your cycle · in 3 of your last 3 cycles"* — arithmetic she could do with a calendar, and no cause, no prediction, no condition named. Three guards on honesty: a symptom must appear in **three distinct cycles** before it is reported (two occurrences are a line, three are a shape); the **running cycle is excluded**, so a symptom that has not happened *yet* is not counted against her; and the **denominator is on screen**, because "days 1–3" alone reads as a rule about her body while "in 3 of your last 3 cycles" lets her weigh it. Carries the reviewed article's own limit verbatim: *a repeating pattern may be useful to record, but the pattern alone cannot confirm a health condition.*

- [x] **R3.3.3** End-of-cycle report (2026-08-22). Both incumbents ship one and `docs/26` §6 lists it as table stakes Femio was missing; it also lands at the one moment in the month when she is looking back rather than forward. **It describes and does not grade** — no score, no "good cycle", no tick for regularity, no arrow saying she improved. `docs/25` §5a rejected the ring because a ring implies a target, and a graded report implies it more explicitly: 31 days is not worse than 28. The only comparison is to **her own earlier cycles**, never a textbook 28, stated as a signed difference and **excluding the cycle being reported** — including it would blunt the very difference the card exists to show. Returns `null` until a cycle has actually completed, since a length is only knowable once the next period starts; guessing it is the prediction's job and carries the prediction's uncertainty. Tier-2, 8 tests. Dashboard **1440px**.

3 · For the judges

Your first GitHub release, step by step

In plain words: a repository is a project folder on GitHub. A release is a page in that repository with a version name (the tag, here v1.0.0-hackathon) and files people can download, here the APK. Judges open the release page on their phone, tap the APK and install it.

My recommendation: make a new, clean public repository femio-app holding only the app (your Devpost text already points there). The export script copies only committed app files and refuses to run if it finds a secret.

Your big Femio repository is public right now. It holds no keys, but it shows your personal e-mail and the internal plans. Once femio-app is live, I recommend making Femio private again: GitHub → Femio → Settings → General → Danger Zone → Change visibility → Private. Vercel keeps deploying the website either way.

What to expect

  • The release page shows your APK plus two automatic "Source code" downloads. GitHub counts downloads per file.
  • On a judge's phone: Chrome warns "file might be harmful" (they tap Download anyway). Android asks to allow installs from Chrome. Play Protect may say "unknown app" or offer a scan (they tap More details → Install anyway). This is normal for every app shared outside Play.
  • "App not installed": a copy from your Play closed test is signed by Google's key, the GitHub copy by yours, so Android refuses to mix them. The fix is to uninstall the first one.
  • Gemini Nano works only on phones with Android AICore (recent Pixels, some Galaxy and other flagships). On the first use the model downloads in the background. On other phones every feature still works on femio's rules.
  • Cost: only Cloud boost calls cost money (Gemini paid tier). Judge traffic is small, and the budget alert covers you.
  • After the deadline, changes you push are not judged. Leave the release as it is until results come out.

5 · Privacy

Is it safe to say "private by design"?

Yes, keep it. Saying it is not what got Flo in trouble. The trouble was saying it while the app did the opposite. In 2021 the US FTC found that Flo promised to keep health data private yet sent users' period and pregnancy events to Facebook and Google analytics through SDKs (FTC case page). Premom got the same treatment in 2023 (FTC). The risk is a promise that the code does not keep.

Why femio is on the safe side: the app has no ads, no analytics and no tracking SDK. Her record stays on the phone. Cloud boost is off by default, asks per purpose, shows the exact text, and keeps a dated receipt. The privacy notice lists everything that can leave the phone, including Google's ML Kit technical usage data and the model download. The lawyer has the full review (sent 27 Nov).

Wording rules that keep you safe

SayAvoid
"Your records stay on your phone.""Nothing ever leaves your phone." (Cloud boost and the model download do, with her choice)
"No account, no ads, no analytics in the app.""100% anonymous", "untraceable", "HIPAA-compliant"
"Cloud boost is off unless you turn it on; you see the exact text first.""Your data is never shared with anyone."
"Private by design" next to one line that says how"Private by design" alone as the whole promise
One line to fix (my recommendation): the website's app page says "Your records never leave your phone." That is true for the record itself, but it reads as absolute next to Cloud boost. I suggest "Your records stay on your phone. Cloud boost is opt-in." Say yes and I will change it in all four languages.

This is guidance, not legal advice. The lawyer's review is the final word.
